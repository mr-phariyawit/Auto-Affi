### Claim check
- Equal coins: rolling coin rotates 2 times. Correct (coin rotation paradox: R/r + 1 = 2).
- 3x coin: 4 rotations, and the 1982 SAT keyed 3. Correct (the SAT item was famously wrong).
- "ANY size: always +1": correct for rolling round the outside of a circle.
- Ladder numbers are self-consistent with a ~26.5 mm coin: Moon 3,475 km / 26.5 mm = ~131 million; Earth 12,742 km -> ~481 million; Sun 1.39 million km -> ~52.5 billion. Plausible (a 26.5 mm coin = US dollar coin). Small-object counters were sampled mid-tick so not checked.
- Earth: 365 solar days = 366 rotations (sidereal), sidereal day 23 h 56 min. Correct (365.25 vs 366.25).

### Differs from the rest of the batch
- Two separate puzzles in one clip (coin round coin, then Earth round Sun) joined by the same "+1" rule; has a "coin cam" inset; ladder of 9 objects is the longest in the batch.

### Why the stronger ones may differ (INFERENCE, not observed fact)
Caveats first: 8 clips is a tiny sample, the clips differ in age by up to 5 days, and I cannot see watch-time, audio, or how TikTok distributed them. The following are only patterns visible in the frames.
- **One big, familiar, tangible object at frame 0.** The two outliers open on a chocolate bar being cut (6) and a single Earth floating inside the Sun (7): one object, large, centred, instantly recognisable. The two weakest open on small or busy stages: two small TV panels with tiny text (8) and a person plus a small cube in a detailed scene (5).
- **A complete pay-off inside ~2 s.** Video 6 finishes a whole cut–slide–"+1 FREE!" cycle by 1.75 s; video 7 has visibly doubled three times by 2 s. Video 8's first real event is at ~3 s and its first pay-off at ~10.8 s; video 5 is static for the first ~0.4 s.
- **The question plugs into something the viewer already believes or has seen.** "Infinite chocolate" is an existing viral trick being debunked; "Earths in the Sun" is a familiar trivia question with a twist (gaps). The mid-table clips pose less familiar puzzles (coin rotation, LCM of bouncing balls).
- **A big readable counter.** Every clip above 100K has a large live number directly under the title; video 8 has none (its numbers are tiny chips).
- **Template consistency.** The weakest two are also the two that deviate most from the house look (5: bright illustrated scene; 8: split-panel layout, no counter). 
- **False-answer beat.** Both top clips contain an explicit wrong answer that is then corrected ("Infinite chocolate?!" -> "The bar SHRANK!"; "FULL!" -> "Round Earths leave gaps!"). Videos 1 and 4 also do this, so it is not sufficient on its own.
- Length does not obviously explain it: the 18 s clip did well, but so did a 26 s one.
- Video 2 (paid-partnership tags) has the highest like rate (3.6%) and save count among the mid-table clips; I cannot tell whether the tags affected its reach.
