## 1. Canonical ingredient list, method, and the documented disputes

### Takeaway
There is no single documented "original" recipe: Thai food historians date the dish to roughly the mid-20th century, and the earliest printed recipes found so far season with fish sauce (plus MSG) only — oyster sauce, soy sauces and sugar are later additions. The "no long beans" purism is a 2013 social-media movement, and at least two older printed recipes actually include or serve long beans, so the channel should present long beans/onion/baby corn/oyster sauce/dark soy as "disputed", not "wrong".

### Cited Findings
- [S] A late-1970s Thai cookbook, ตำราอาหารชุดจัดสำรับ (ชุด 2), seasons beef pad kra pao with fish sauce and MSG only ("ผัดกะเพราเนื้อจะปรุงด้วยน้ำปลาและผงชูรสเท่านั้น") and serves it with fresh long beans ("กินกับถั่วฝักยาวสด"). — [Sanook Campus](https://www.sanook.com/campus/1415627/)
- [S] A 1970 (พ.ศ. 2513) recipe circulated online (described as from a cooking handbook) uses minced chicken, fish sauce (3 tbsp), MSG (½ tsp), roasted ground dried bird's-eye chilli (2 tsp), fresh red chilli, garlic, lard or vegetable oil, holy basil — and lists long beans cut ~1 inch. — [Sanook Campus](https://www.sanook.com/campus/1415627/)
- [S] A 1998 (พ.ศ. 2541) book by "แม่ครัวเอก", กับแกล้มเหล้า–เบียร์ทันยุค, marinates the mince in liquor then seasons with fish sauce and palm sugar only ("ปรุงเพียงน้ำปลาและน้ำตาลปี๊บ"); the article says later versions added more sauces, granulated sugar, oyster sauce and chilli paste. — [Sanook Campus](https://www.sanook.com/campus/1415627/)
- [S] Holy basil itself is attested much earlier than the dish: La Loubère (1687) lists it among fragrant vegetables and Bradley's 1873 dictionary อักขราภิธานศรับท์ describes it as a small plant with fragrant leaves used in curries and medicine. — [Sanook Campus](https://www.sanook.com/campus/1415627/)
- [U] Food writer Krit Lueamlamai (กฤช เหลือลมัย) writes that cookbooks of a century ago never mention phat phrik bai kaphrao, that elders' recollections reach back only to about พ.ศ. 2500 (c. 1957), and that he believes it began with Chinese cooks at made-to-order shops. Search snippet only; page returned 403. — [Matichon](https://www.matichon.co.th/lifestyle/news_827757)
- [U] Silpa-Mag reports (citing Narit Charatchanyawong, นริศ จรัสจรรยาวงศ์) that the oldest recorded recipe found is "เนื้อวัวผัดกะเพรา" by a head of the royal kitchen of King Rama IX, connected to the period after 1951 (พ.ศ. 2494). Snippet only; page returned 403; recipe details not seen. — [Silpa-Mag](https://www.silpa-mag.com/?p=148492)
- [U] The Facebook page "ทวงคืนผัดกะเพราไม่ใส่ถั่วฝักยาว ข้าวโพดอ่อน และหัวหอมใหญ่" ("Reclaim pad kra pao without long beans, baby corn and onion") started the public purism debate in พ.ศ. 2556 (2013). Search snippet; originating article not opened (appears in the Matichon/Thairath result set). — [Matichon](https://www.matichon.co.th/lifestyle/news_827757)
- [S] Modern street-style recipe (Mark Wiens, Bangkok-based food writer, not Thai): 200 g chicken, 5 garlic cloves, 4–10 Thai chillies, 1 tsp oyster sauce, ½ tsp light soy, a splash of dark soy, ½ tsp sugar, a handful of holy basil, 1 tbsp oil; no vegetables listed. — [Eating Thai Food](https://www.eatingthaifood.com/thai-basil-chicken-recipe-pad-kra-pao-gai/)
- [U] Thai-born cook Praew (Hungry in Thailand; ran a Thai restaurant in Belgium): sauce is light soy, oyster sauce, fish sauce and a little sugar; "some people add dark soy sauce ... but my mom and I always leave it out". Search snippet of her recipe page. — [Hungry in Thailand](https://hungryinthailand.com/pad-kra-pao/)
- [U] Pailin Chongchitnant (Hot Thai Kitchen) lists long beans as a favourite vegetable to add. Search snippet; page returned 402. — [Hot Thai Kitchen](https://hot-thai-kitchen.com/pad-kra-pao-anything)

## 2. Holy basil (กะเพรา) — taxonomy, Thai types, chemistry, look-alikes

### Takeaway
Accepted name is *Ocimum tenuiflorum* L. (*O. sanctum* L. is a synonym). Thai red and white/green types differ in pigment and aroma, but the chemistry is NOT a clean "red = eugenol, white = something else" story: studies conflict, eugenol and methyl eugenol trade off against each other, and composition depends on accession and growing site — so on-screen wording should be "clove-like (eugenol / methyl eugenol)" without percentages attached to a colour.

### Cited Findings
**Taxonomy**
- [P] *Ocimum tenuiflorum* L. is ACCEPTED; *Ocimum sanctum* L. is a SYNONYM of it (GBIF Backbone Taxonomy). — [GBIF: O. tenuiflorum](https://www.gbif.org/species/2927100); [GBIF: O. sanctum](https://www.gbif.org/species/2927101)
- [P] *Ocimum basilicum* var. *thyrsiflorum* (L.) Benth. (the name often given for Thai sweet basil, โหระพา) is treated as a SYNONYM of *Ocimum basilicum* in the GBIF backbone. — [GBIF](https://www.gbif.org/species/8008078)
- [P] *Ocimum × africanum* Lour. is ACCEPTED and *Ocimum × citriodorum* Vis. is its SYNONYM (the names used for lemon basil, แมงลัก). — [GBIF: O. × africanum](https://www.gbif.org/species/3898584); [GBIF: O. × citriodorum](https://www.gbif.org/species/3898086)

**Thai types and chemistry**
- [P] Thai holy basil accessions differ in "stem color, stem pubescence appearance, leaf vein color, sepal color"; the commercial red (R) cultivar had the highest methyl eugenol (3,357.27 µg/g DW) while four other Thai accessions had the highest eugenol (c. 4,146–4,581 µg/g DW); methyl eugenol and eugenol were strongly negatively correlated. Plants were grown in a plant factory (hydroponic, artificial light), seeds of the commercial red and green types from Chia Tai Co., Bangkok. — [Chutimanukul et al. 2022, Frontiers in Plant Science 13, doi 10.3389/fpls.2022.1008917](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/plant-science/articles/10.3389/fpls.2022.1008917/full)
- [P] Same paper's discussion: earlier work found that green and red holy basil in Thailand both have methyl eugenol as the major leaf compound. — [Chutimanukul et al. 2022](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/plant-science/articles/10.3389/fpls.2022.1008917/full)
- [U] Chulalongkorn University thesis: white holy basil leaves have eugenol as the only key odorant, whereas red holy basil has both eugenol and methyl eugenol; red is greener, more pungent and mintier, less citrus-like. Snippet only; repository unreachable. — [Chula CUIR 82970](https://cuir.car.chula.ac.th/xmlui/handle/123456789/82970?show=full)
- [U] Other Thai essential-oil studies report methyl eugenol as the main component of BOTH types (e.g. 54.29% white / 51.21% red in one; 53.67% white / 47.18% red in another); a Phitsanulok sample was 76.3% methyl eugenol with 9.9% β-caryophyllene; a 1972 analysis of Thai-grown oil reported 71.3% eugenol / 20.4% methyl eugenol. All snippet-level; individual papers not opened. — [KMUTNB IJST](https://ojs.kmutnb.ac.th/index.php/ijst/article/download/6797/pdf_434); [AHSTR (TCI-ThaiJO)](https://ph03.tci-thaijo.org/index.php/ahstr/article/view/2399); [Perfumer & Flavorist review](https://img.perfumerflavorist.com/files/base/allured/all/document/2015/04/pf.PF_40_05_039_11.pdf)
- [U] Thai Dept. of Thai Traditional and Alternative Medicine herb sheet states the kind and amount of constituents depend on where the plant is grown. Snippet; PDF host did not resolve. — [DTAM herb sheet 0010](https://abdul.dtam.moph.go.th/thaiherbs/herb_pdf/0010.pdf)
- [U] Kasetsart (Kamphaeng Saen) work notes eugenol accumulation in the purple-red leaves of red holy basil. Snippet. — [TJNPR](https://tjnpr.org/index.php/home/article/download/5632/5245/12478)

**Visual identification and look-alikes (culinary authorities)**
- [S] Holy basil: leaves "somewhat soft and fuzzy" with saw-tooth edges; leaves green or purple; hairy stems; small shrub to about 3 ft; flavour like Italian basil crossed with mint plus clove and a black-pepper aftertaste; purple-leafed variety is stronger. — [Rachel Cooks Thai](https://rachelcooksthai.com/holy-basil/)
- [S] Thai sweet basil (horapha): narrow green leaves, purple stems, liquorice-like taste. — [Rachel Cooks Thai](https://rachelcooksthai.com/holy-basil/)
- [S] Thai cook Praew: holy basil leaves "jagged, soft", Thai basil leaves smooth and shiny with purple stems; holy basil exists in green-stemmed and purple-stemmed forms, purple being more pungent; holy basil is "spicy, peppery, clove-like", Thai basil "sweet, slightly licorice-like". — [Hungry in Thailand](https://hungryinthailand.com/holy-basil-vs-thai-basil/)
- [S] Lemon basil is used in khanom chin nam ya and kaeng liang (not pad kra pao). — [Rachel Cooks Thai](https://rachelcooksthai.com/holy-basil/)

**Substitutes abroad**
- [S] "No good substitute"; Thai or Italian basil can stand in but the dish becomes a different dish (pad horapha); dried leaves and seasoning pastes compared poorly with fresh. Buy at shops serving Thai/SE Asian communities (summer–early autumn) or grow from seed; not frost tolerant. — [Rachel Cooks Thai](https://rachelcooksthai.com/holy-basil/)
- [S] Mark Wiens: sweet basil lacks holy basil's "vibrant peppery flavor"; US Thai restaurants commonly substitute it. — [Eating Thai Food](https://www.eatingthaifood.com/thai-basil-chicken-recipe-pad-kra-pao-gai/)
- [U] Pailin Chongchitnant: Italian basil is her preferred substitute, closer in flavour than Thai basil. Search snippet; page returned 402. — [Hot Thai Kitchen](https://hot-thai-kitchen.com/pad-kra-pao-anything)

### Inferences
- For the illustrator, the sourced distinguishing set is: holy basil = matte, soft, fuzzy leaf with clearly toothed margin + hairy stem (green, or purple-flushed in the red type); Thai sweet basil = smooth glossy narrow leaf + purple hairless-looking stem. Leaf vein, stem and sepal colour are the documented axes of variation between Thai accessions (Frontiers 2022).
- "Red = stronger" is supported only by cooks' statements [S]; lab data is inconsistent. Say "many Thai cooks consider the red type more pungent", not "red has more eugenol".

### Gaps
- **กะเพราป่า ("wild" kaphrao): no reliable source found** for its botanical identity, appearance, aroma or growing region. Do not describe it on screen beyond "a market name for a strongly scented type" until a source is found.
- **Precise botanical drawing details not sourced**: leaf shape and dimensions, petiole, inflorescence/flower-spike structure, flower colour, seed. Commonly stated (elliptic-ovate leaves a few cm long, slender spikes of small whorled flowers, purplish in red type) — all UNVERIFIED here. Check Flora of Thailand (Lamiaceae), the DTAM sheet above, or Kew POWO (403 to my client: https://powo.science.kew.org/).
- Kew POWO could not be opened; GBIF backbone used instead. Verify author citations and the status of var. *thyrsiflorum* against POWO before printing Latin names.
- No source found for main holy-basil growing provinces in Thailand.
- No botanical description of lemon basil (แมงลัก) appearance from a credible source.

## 3. Chillies (พริก)

### Takeaway
Thai government sheets group cooking chillies as large-fruited bird's-eye (the Jinda type), small-fruited bird's-eye (khi nu suan type) and large chillies (chi fa type) with named growing provinces, but I found no primary Scoville figure for phrik khi nu suan, phrik chinda or phrik chi fa by those market names. The only peer-reviewed heat data found is by research cultivar codes: most Thai chilli cultivars tested fall at 45,000–80,000 SHU, two exceed 80,000.

### Cited Findings
- [P] Department of Agriculture crop sheet for chilli gives family Solanaceae, genus *Capsicum*, species *frutescens* L., and lists Thai types: พริกขี้หนูผลใหญ่, พริกชี้ฟ้า, พริกขี้หนูสวน, พริกหยวก, พริกหวาน. — [DOA plant sheet 76](https://doaplant.doa.go.th/GardFront/PlantDetailPdf/76)
- [P] Growing provinces: large-fruited bird's-eye — Chaiyaphum, Si Sa Ket, Ubon Ratchathani, Nakhon Phanom, Tak, Chiang Mai, Phetchabun, Kanchanaburi, Nakhon Si Thammarat; small-fruited bird's-eye — Tak, Kanchanaburi, Chiang Mai, Nakhon Si Thammarat; large chillies — Phrae, Chiang Mai, Nakhon Ratchasima, Kanchanaburi. — [DOA plant sheet 76](https://doaplant.doa.go.th/GardFront/PlantDetailPdf/76)
- [U] DOA variety leaflet: the large-seeded bird's-eye chilli called "พริกจินดา" was trialled at the Huai Si Thon model-farm project (Kalasin) and selected into the variety Huai Si Thon 1; fruit points upward, ripens deep red, about 4 cm long. Snippet; PDF not opened. — [DOA: พริกขี้หนูพันธุ์ กวก. ห้วยสีทน 1](https://www.doa.go.th/hort/wp-content/uploads/2024/12/พริกขี้หนูพันธุ์-กวก.-ห้วยสีทน-1.pdf)
- [P] Kraikruan, Sukprakarn, Mongkolporn & Wasee (2008), Kasetsart J. (Agriculture and Natural Resources) 42(4): 611–616 — ten Thai cultivars (six *C. annuum*, four *C. frutescens*) by HPLC: capsaicin 0.76–3.76 mg/g, dihydrocapsaicin 0.59–2.39 mg/g; all except K05 "highly pungent" at 45,000–80,000 SHU; Huarua (*C. annuum*) and K07 (*C. frutescens*) "very highly pungent" at >80,000 SHU; Huarua, Huayseeton SK1 (*C. annuum*) and K07 had >5.2 mg/g capsaicinoids; content was highest at first harvest. — [Kraikruan et al. 2008](https://li01.tci-thaijo.org/index.php/anres/article/view/244492)
- [P] *Capsicum annuum* L. and *Capsicum frutescens* L. are both accepted species names. — [GBIF: C. annuum](https://www.gbif.org/species/2932944); [GBIF: C. frutescens](https://www.gbif.org/species/8403992)
- [U] Generic "bird's eye chilli 50,000–100,000 SHU" appears in Wikipedia's Scoville table. Snippet; tertiary source. — [Wikipedia: Scoville scale](https://www.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scoville)
- [S] Recipe usage: "4–10 Thai chilies" per single serving. — [Eating Thai Food](https://www.eatingthaifood.com/thai-basil-chicken-recipe-pad-kra-pao-gai/)

### Inferences
- "Huayseeton SK1" in Kraikruan et al. is very likely the Huai Si Thon (Jinda-derived) line, which the authors class as *C. annuum* — this would mean the Jinda type is *C. annuum* even though the DOA general sheet files chilli under *C. frutescens*. This is my inference from name similarity; not confirmed.
- Because the species assignment of Thai market types is inconsistent between sources, the safest on-screen Latin is "*Capsicum* spp." or to name the species only for a specific cited cultivar.

## 4. Garlic (กระเทียม)

### Takeaway
Thai garlic is *Allium sativum* L. grown mainly in the upper North, with Si Sa Ket the notable north-eastern origin; the Department of Agriculture distinguishes an early white "light" type (e.g. Si Sa Ket local, pungent and strong-tasting), a purple-skinned mid-season northern type, and a late large-bulb, large-clove pink-skinned Chinese/Taiwanese type. Kratiam Si Sa Ket is reported as a registered Thai GI (2020), but I only saw this in secondary snippets.

### Cited Findings
- [P] Family Amaryllidaceae, *Allium sativum* L. Main production in the upper North: Chiang Mai, Lamphun, Chiang Rai, Mae Hong Son, Phayao, Nan, Phrae, Lampang, Uttaradit; also the Northeast, e.g. Si Sa Ket and Buri Ram. — [DOA plant sheet 133](https://doaplant.doa.go.th/GardFront/PlantDetailPdf/133)
- [P] Varieties farmers grow: North — Chiang Mai local, Chiang Rai local, "Burmese"; Northeast — Si Sa Ket local; Central — Bang Chang and Chinese/Taiwanese. — [DOA plant sheet 133](https://doaplant.doa.go.th/GardFront/PlantDetailPdf/133)
- [P] By maturity: (1) light / "khao mueang" type — pointed leaves, cloves and bulb white, "มีกลิ่นฉุนและรสจัด" (pungent aroma, strong taste), 75–90 days, e.g. Si Sa Ket local; (2) medium — medium bulb, purple bulb and cloves, 90–120 days, popular in the North, e.g. Chiang Mai local; (3) heavy — "หัวใหญ่ กลีบโต เปลือกหุ้มสีชมพู" (large bulb, big cloves, pink skin), about 150 days, e.g. Chinese or Taiwanese variety. — [DOA plant sheet 133](https://doaplant.doa.go.th/GardFront/PlantDetailPdf/133)
- [P] Planted area: North 80,853 rai, Northeast 1,088 rai, national 81,941 rai (year not stated on the lines read). — [DOA plant sheet 133](https://doaplant.doa.go.th/GardFront/PlantDetailPdf/133)
- [U] "กระเทียมศรีสะเกษ" (Kratiam Si Sa Ket) was registered as a Thai GI together with Si Sa Ket shallots on 18 June 2020; registration number cited as สช 63100143. Snippets from a journal article and a blog post; DIP database not opened. — [SSKRU research journal](https://so01.tci-thaijo.org/index.php/sskru-resarch/article/view/283749); [Blockdit post](https://www.blockdit.com/posts/5fd9ebeecf19720ccc94f5d2)

### Inferences
- The "small-clove Thai vs large-clove Chinese" contrast is supported by DOA for the Chinese side ("กลีบโต") and for pungency of the Thai light type; DOA's wording for the Thai light type's clove size ("กลีบเท่าหัวแม่มือ") is odd and should not be quoted as "small".

### Gaps
- GI status must be confirmed on the Department of Intellectual Property GI register (ipthailand.go.th) before on-screen use; I did not open a DIP page. No check made for other garlic GIs (e.g. northern provinces).
- No primary source found stating that Thai garlic cloves are smaller, or comparing allicin/pungency with Chinese garlic.
- No authority page opened on whether to peel Thai garlic for pad kra pao or on substitutes abroad.

## 5. Sauces — fish sauce, oyster sauce, thin and black soy

### Takeaway
Fish sauce is the one sauce with a fully verified legal definition: Thai law defines three types (genuine, from other animals, mixed) with minimum salt and nitrogen levels. Soy and oyster sauce definitions and brand facts rest on snippets from cooking sites only and need re-checking.

### Cited Findings
**Fish sauce (น้ำปลา)**
- [P] Ministry of Public Health Notification No. 203 (พ.ศ. 2543 / 2000) "เรื่อง น้ำปลา" repealed Notification No. 118 (1989); it excludes budu. Fish sauce is defined as a salty liquid used to flavour food, in three types: (1) น้ำปลาแท้ "genuine fish sauce" — from fermenting or digesting fish, fish parts or residue from fermentation; (2) น้ำปลาที่ทำจากสัตว์อื่น — from animals other than fish; (3) น้ำปลาผสม "mixed fish sauce" — type 1 or 2 with other harmless substances added, diluted or flavoured. — [Thai FDA consolidated Notification 203](https://exfood.fda.moph.go.th/law/data/complilation/Compi_203.pdf)
- [P] Standards for genuine fish sauce: sodium chloride ≥ 200 g/L; total nitrogen ≥ 9 g/L; amino-acid nitrogen 40–60% of total nitrogen; glutamic acid : total nitrogen 0.4–0.6; clear, natural sediment ≤ 0.1 g/L. Mixed fish sauce: total nitrogen ≥ 4 g/L; glutamic acid : total nitrogen 0.4–1.3; same salt minimum. — [Thai FDA consolidated Notification 203](https://exfood.fda.moph.go.th/law/data/complilation/Compi_203.pdf)
- [P] Labelling: type 1 must be named "น้ำปลาแท้"; type 3 must be named "น้ำปลาผสม". Iodine clause (2–3 mg/L where iodine is added) was inserted by the 2010 amendment (No. 2). — [Thai FDA consolidated Notification 203](https://exfood.fda.moph.go.th/law/data/complilation/Compi_203.pdf)
- [P] The Thai FDA's fish-sauce law page lists Notification 203 (2000), the No. 2 amendment, and Notification No. 403 (พ.ศ. 2562 / 2019) "น้ำปลา (ฉบับที่ 3)". — [Thai FDA food law: fish sauce](https://food.fda.moph.go.th/food-law/f2-fish-sauce)
- [U] An industrial standard มอก. 3-2562 for fish sauce also exists. Snippet (WTO TBT notification / news). — [WTO TBT attachment](https://members.wto.org/crnattachments/2024/TBT/THA/24_00746_00_x.pdf)
- [U] Thai production typically uses small marine anchovy-group fish such as ปลาไส้ตัน / ปลากะตัก, salted at roughly 2:1 to 5:1 fish:salt by weight. Snippet from a news feature. — [Bangkok Biz News](https://www.bangkokbiznews.com/health/well-being/1082883)
- [U] JIRCAS Thai fermented foods database: nam pla uses *Stolephorus* spp. (incl. *S. indicus*), fermentation about 18 months, shelf life about 2 years. Snippet. — [JIRCAS: nam plaa](https://www.jircas.go.jp/en/database/thaifermented/nam-plaa)
- [U] Department of Fisheries document: anchovies caught in the Gulf of Thailand fall in two economically important genera, *Encrasicholina* and *Stolephorus*. Snippet. — [Dept. of Fisheries](https://www4.fisheries.go.th/local/file_announce/202406261056331_announce.pdf)

**Soy sauces and oyster sauce**
- [U] ซีอิ๊วขาว (thin/white soy) is a liquid from fermenting soybean protein with no added flavouring or colour, in two quality grades; salty black soy (ซีอิ๊วดำเค็ม) is thin soy aged further to a set concentration and colour. Snippet from Thai agricultural-technology features; no legal text opened. — [Khaosod Technology Chaoban](https://www.khaosod.co.th/technologychaoban/?p=19825)
- [U] Thai black soy is slightly sweet from added molasses/palm-sugar molasses, thicker, used mainly for colour, and differs from Chinese dark soy; Thai thin soy is made from fermented soybeans and wheat; Thai oyster sauce is "usually slightly lighter and sweeter" than Chinese. Snippets from cooking sites; pages not opened. — [The Woks of Life: Thai soy sauce](https://thewoksoflife.com/thai-soy-sauce/); [Real Thai Recipes: sauces & pastes](https://www.realthairecipes.com/thai-ingredient-glossary/sauces-and-pastes/); [Messy Vegan Cook](https://messyvegancook.com/different-types-of-thai-soy-sauces-and-condiments/)
- [U] Brands named in those snippets (factual mention, not endorsement): Healthy Boy / เด็กสมบูรณ์ (thin soy; described as the Thai soy most often found abroad), Golden Mountain (seasoning sauce, green cap), Maekrua and Megachef (oyster sauce), Squid, Tiparos and Megachef (fish sauce). Not verified against manufacturer or export data. — [The Woks of Life](https://thewoksoflife.com/thai-soy-sauce/); [Real Thai Recipes](https://www.realthairecipes.com/thai-ingredient-glossary/sauces-and-pastes/)

### Inferences
- A defensible caption: "By Thai law, a bottle labelled น้ำปลาแท้ must contain at least 9 g/L total nitrogen and 200 g/L salt; น้ำปลาผสม may be as low as 4 g/L nitrogen." This lets viewers read Thai labels abroad.
- Thai law defines types, not "grades" in the Vietnamese °N sense; avoid the word "grade" for fish sauce unless the TIS (มอก. 3) grade table is checked.

## 6. Jasmine rice (ข้าวหอมมะลิ)

### Takeaway
Khao Hom Mali Thung Kula Rong-Hai is a verified EU PGI (registered 12 February 2013) and UK-protected name, grown only from Khao Dawk Mali 105 and RD15 in five north-eastern provinces. "Thai Hom Mali" as a general trade name is tied to the same two varieties, with a 92% purity threshold reported in secondary sources only.

### Cited Findings
- [P] "ข้าวหอมมะลิทุ่งกุลาร้องไห้ / Khao Hom Mali Thung Kula Rong-Hai": PGI, status Registered, class 1.6, country Thailand; original EU registration date 12 February 2013; carried into the UK scheme 31 December 2020. — [UK GOV protected food names](https://www.gov.uk/protected-food-drink-names/khaawhmmalithungkulaarngaih-slash-khao-hom-mali-thung-kula-rong-hai)
- [U] EU legal act: Commission Implementing Regulation (EU) No 120/2013 of 11 February 2013; application from Thailand received 20 November 2008; EC No TH-PGI-0005-0729-20.11.2008. From search snippet of EUR-Lex; act itself not opened; eAmbrosia not opened. — [EUR-Lex Reg. 120/2013](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg_impl/2013/120/oj/eng/pdf)
- [P] Product specification: grown from "Khao Dawk Mali (KDML 105) and Kor Khor or 'RD' 15 varieties", a photo-sensitive rain-fed paddy; may be brown or white rice; hull straw-yellow; grain long, slim, translucent; smell likened to pandan leaf. — [PGI specification (UK GOV copy)](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/6658506ed470e3279dd333b3/Khao_Hom_MAli_Thung_Kula_Rong-Hai.pdf)
- [P] Specification numbers: grain length > 7.0 mm; length/width > 3.2; amylose 14–16%; moisture ≤ 14%; mixture of other rice varieties ≤ 8%; alkali test 6–7. — [PGI specification (UK GOV copy)](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/6658506ed470e3279dd333b3/Khao_Hom_MAli_Thung_Kula_Rong-Hai.pdf)
- [P] Area: five provinces — Roi Et (986,807 rai), Surin (575,993 rai), Si Sa Ket (287,000 rai), Maha Sarakham (193,890 rai), Yasothon (64,000 rai); planted April–August, harvested October–December; production and processing must take place in those provinces; seed from the Rice Department or certified producers. — [PGI specification (UK GOV copy)](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/6658506ed470e3279dd333b3/Khao_Hom_MAli_Thung_Kula_Rong-Hai.pdf)
- [U] Thai GI registration number for the same name: สช 50100022. Snippet. — [WIPO IP Advantage](https://www.wipo.int/web/ip-advantage/w/stories/thai-hom-mali-rice-from-thung-kula-rong-hai-a-world-recognized-gi)
- [U] Thai Agricultural Standard TAS 4000-2017 (มกษ. 4000-2560) "Thai Hom Mali Rice" recognises Khao Dawk Mali 105 and RD15 as the varieties. Snippet; PDF not opened. — [TAS 4000-2017 (ALRO copy)](https://alro.go.th/uploads/org/alro_th/article_attach/article_attach_201901291548730780.pdf)
- [U] The standard requires not less than 92% Thai Hom Mali rice by quantity under the English name "THAI HOM MALI RICE". News snippet. — [Thansettakij](https://www.thansettakij.com/business/105310)
- [U] The Department of Foreign Trade (Ministry of Commerce) owns the Thai Hom Mali rice certification mark, registered with the DIP since 1991, and reports registration in 48 countries, renewed every 10 years. News snippet. — [Thairath](https://www.thairath.co.th/money/economics/thai_economics/2656111)

### Inferences
- The PGI spec's "other varieties ≤ 8%" matches the reported 92% purity rule, which makes the 92% figure plausible but still not primary-verified for the general Thai Hom Mali standard.
- On screen: "Thung Kula Rong-Hai jasmine rice — protected in the EU since 2013" is safe; "Thai Hom Mali = at least 92% KDML 105 / RD15" needs the Ministry of Commerce rice-standard text.

### Gaps
- eAmbrosia record not opened (current EU status assumed unchanged from the UK GOV page's stated EU date).
- 2-acetyl-1-pyrroline as the aroma compound: not found in the lines of the spec I read; unsourced here.
- Substitutes abroad (e.g. other long-grain rice) and what is lost: no authority opened.

## 8. Substitutes abroad — summary of what is sourced

### Takeaway
Only holy basil has substitute guidance I could read from culinary authorities; all agree nothing truly replaces it, with Italian basil (per Pailin, unverified) or Thai basil as fallbacks that change the dish.

### Cited Findings
- [S] Holy basil → Thai or Italian basil; loses the peppery, clove-like heat; dish effectively becomes pad horapha. Dried holy basil and jarred pastes rated poorly. — [Rachel Cooks Thai](https://rachelcooksthai.com/holy-basil/)
- [S] Holy basil → sweet basil "won't have that peppery flavor" but still tastes good. — [Eating Thai Food](https://www.eatingthaifood.com/thai-basil-chicken-recipe-pad-kra-pao-gai/)
- [S] Thai-born cook Praew: holy and Thai basil "are not interchangeable" for her. — [Hungry in Thailand](https://hungryinthailand.com/holy-basil-vs-thai-basil/)
- [U] Italian basil preferred over Thai basil as substitute (Pailin Chongchitnant). — [Hot Thai Kitchen](https://hot-thai-kitchen.com/pad-kra-pao-anything)
- [S] Dark soy → Indonesian kecap manis suggested as an option. — [Eating Thai Food](https://www.eatingthaifood.com/thai-basil-chicken-recipe-pad-kra-pao-gai/)

### Inferences
- None beyond the above.

### Gaps
- No credible-authority substitute guidance found for Thai chillies, Thai garlic, Thai fish sauce, thin soy or jasmine rice.
