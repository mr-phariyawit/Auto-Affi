## 1. Flavour target — how Thai sources describe it, old-style vs modern vs abroad, and a general Thai balance framework

### Takeaway
The sources that describe the taste agree on the order of importance: holy-basil aroma first, then chilli heat and garlic, salted mainly with fish sauce; sweetness is a background rounding note, not a feature. No Thai authority read gives a numeric "balance"; the dish is not sour at all in the pan — sourness arrives only at the table through prik nam pla with lime.

### Cited Findings
**Krit Lueamlamai (กฤช เหลือลมัย), Thai food writer**
- [P, browser] The essential thing is the basil: "แต่ขึ้นชื่อว่าผัดกะเพรา สิ่งสำคัญย่อมคือ 'กะเพรา'". He thinks Thais still prefer the pungent, hot type over the sweet-smelling mild type ("พันธุ์ฉุนแรงเผ็ดร้อน มากกว่าพันธุ์ฉุนหวานกลิ่นอ่อน"), but market basil is mostly large, soft-leaved and less good. — [Matichon, 3 Feb 2018](https://www.matichon.co.th/lifestyle/news_827757)
- [P, browser] His target in his own words: "เผ็ดโหดรสจัดจ้าน หอมกลิ่นใบกะเพราฉุนๆ ไม่มีกลิ่นซอส ฯลฯ มารบกวน" (fiercely hot, strongly seasoned, fragrant with pungent holy basil, with no sauce smell interfering). — [Matichon, 3 Feb 2018](https://www.matichon.co.th/lifestyle/news_827757)
- [P, browser] His criticism of the common modern plate: "กะเพราไก่ไข่ดาวน้ำมันเยิ้ม อุดมซอสสารพัดชนิดจนหากลิ่นกะเพราไม่เจอ" (oily, loaded with so many sauces that you cannot find the basil smell). — [Matichon, 3 Feb 2018](https://www.matichon.co.th/lifestyle/news_827757)
- [P, browser] "หัวใจของผัดกะเพรา… คือ 'ใบกะเพรา' ใบเล็กๆ ฉุนร้อน กลิ่นหอมแรง และมีเนื้อใบค่อนข้างหนา" (the heart of the dish is small, hot-pungent, strongly fragrant, fairly thick leaves). — [Matichon, 21 Dec 2019](https://www.matichon.co.th/lifestyle/news_1827717)
- [P, browser] Of a retro version seasoned with fish sauce only: without seasoning sauce, white sugar, oyster sauce or black soy, "กลิ่นพริกเหลือง กระเทียม น้ำปลา และ… กลิ่นฉุนของใบกะเพราดีๆ จะโดดเด่นรุนแรงมาก" — a taste he says cannot be ordered in any restaurant in Thailand today. — [Matichon, 21 Dec 2019](https://www.matichon.co.th/lifestyle/news_1827717)

**Old-style (โบราณ) vs modern — note that "old-style" means two different things**
- [P, browser] Krit, citing N. Na Paknam: the earliest cook-shop version (c. B.E. 2500) was a high-heat Chinese-cook stir-fry seasoned with black bean sauce or else "ซีอิ๊วดำหวาน-ดำเค็ม ร่วมกับน้ำปลา"; mild heat from sliced chi fa chillies; ground pepper on top. — [Matichon 2019](https://www.matichon.co.th/lifestyle/news_1827717); [Matichon 2018](https://www.matichon.co.th/lifestyle/news_827757)
- [P, browser] Krit's "half-century recipe" is adapted from two printed books: หนังสือจัดสำรับ ชุด 2 (2519 / 1976), recipe by คุณจิตต์สมาน โกมลฐิติ, and ประมวลกับแกล้มเหล้า-เบียร์ทันยุค (2541 / 1998). Salted with fish sauce only; the 1976 book suggests MSG (he omits it); eaten with raw long beans on the side. — [Matichon 2019](https://www.matichon.co.th/lifestyle/news_1827717)
- [P, browser] Pailin Chongchitnant: the modern version (the one a local Thai restaurant probably makes) "contains veggies like green beans and onions; along with a more complex seasoning that might include oyster sauce, soy sauce, and dark soy sauce"; the old-school style is "much simpler" — "Fish sauce is your only seasoning in traditional pad kra pao". — [Hot Thai Kitchen, beef](https://hot-thai-kitchen.com/pad-kra-pao-beef/)
- [P, browser] Krit's own caution about "authentic": each generation grew up with a different version and remembers a different "ความจริงแท้"; in other fields of Thai studies "แท้" is understood to be hard to find, even a myth. — [Matichon 2019](https://www.matichon.co.th/lifestyle/news_1827717)

**Heat level**
- [P, browser] Pailin: "Pad kra pao is supposed to be a spicy dish so put as many chilies as you can tolerate"; Thai chillies give heat, mild red chillies (traditionally phrik chi fa) give "colour and flavour". — [Hot Thai Kitchen, anything](https://hot-thai-kitchen.com/pad-kra-pao-anything/); [Hot Thai Kitchen, beef](https://hot-thai-kitchen.com/pad-kra-pao-beef/)

**Sweetness** (see matrix in `ingredient_cards_gapfill.md` §6)
- [S] Sugar is present but small in M.L. Tiw 1954 (1 ช้อนคาว to 2 cups beef) and Thompson ("large pinch of white sugar"); Leela Punyaratabandhu: "I don't like sugar in my Pad Ka-Prao"; Krit lists none. — [Silpa-Mag](https://www.silpa-mag.com/culture/article_148492); [Gourmet Traveller](https://www.gourmettraveller.com.au/recipe/chefs-recipes/david-thompson-stir-fried-minced-beef-with-chillies-and-holy-basil-neua-pat-bai-grapao-7362/); [SheSimmers](https://shesimmers.com/2012/05/pad-ka-prao-%e0%b8%9c%e0%b8%b1%e0%b8%94%e0%b8%81%e0%b8%b0%e0%b9%80%e0%b8%9e%e0%b8%a3%e0%b8%b2.html)
- [P, browser] Pailin's old-school beef recipe is the sweetest of the set: 1 tbsp granulated sugar to 12 oz beef with 1 tbsp + 2 tsp fish sauce and ½ cup stock. — [Hot Thai Kitchen, beef](https://hot-thai-kitchen.com/pad-kra-pao-beef/)

**Dry vs saucy**
- [P, browser] Pailin adds half the stock first, and "if it's looking too dry, add a splash more water or stock" — i.e. a little sauce to moisten rice, not a gravy. Thompson uses ¼ cup stock or water [S]. Krit's and M.L. Tiw's methods add no water at all [P/S]. — [Hot Thai Kitchen, beef](https://hot-thai-kitchen.com/pad-kra-pao-beef/); [Matichon 2018](https://www.matichon.co.th/lifestyle/news_827757)

**Versions abroad**
- [P, fetch] Michelin Guide Thailand (James Whiting, 28 Oct 2021) on Japan: restaurants often switch to sweet basil (โหระพา), "which lacks the same peppery heat as the original"; coriander is piled on; chicken is the default; Japanese/Korean-style light soy replaces Thai sauces. The same article describes holy basil's aroma as clove with cinnamon and mint notes. — [Michelin Guide TH](https://guide.michelin.com/th/th/article/features/phad-kaprao---how-the-japanese-have-embraced-the-thai-favourite)
- [P, browser] Pailin: outside Thailand restaurants "might be actually be using Thai basil because holy basil is unavailable". — [Hot Thai Kitchen, beef](https://hot-thai-kitchen.com/pad-kra-pao-beef/)

**General Thai flavour-balance framework**
- [P, fetch] Thailand Foundation (5 Sep 2022): "The secret of Thai food goes to the balance of the taste in the dish"; fish sauce sets saltiness and adds umami; prik nam pla is described as sour, salty, sweet and spicy. — [Thailand Foundation](https://thailandfoundation.or.th/?p=512794)
- [P, fetch] David Thompson (interview by Sudi Pigott, 22 Nov 2024): flavours "work harmoniously in concert: rounding, contrasting, and supporting each other"; he trains cooks "to distinguish the primary taste and the sequence of taste" and to keep asking "is it more sweet, or salty, or both?"; "it is always an interplay of taste and texture plus deft seasoning". — [Fine Dining Lovers](https://www.finedininglovers.co.uk/explore/articles/david-thompson-interview)
- [U] Thompson elsewhere: every dish should balance sweet, sour, hot and salty, achieved "by tasting, tasting and tasting"; and balance applies across the dishes of a meal as well as within one dish. Snippets. — [Fine Dining Lovers (search result)](https://finedininglovers.com/node/20936); [Good Food](https://www.goodfood.com.au/recipes/tips-for-aspiring-thai-cooks-20131008-2v5r8)
- [U] Thai-language popular sources list the taste sources: salt from fish sauce, salt and thin soy; sweet from several sugars; sour from lime, tamarind, vinegar; heat from chillies; richness from coconut milk and oil. Snippet. — [Wongnai](https://www.wongnai.com/food-tips/7-identities-of-thai-food)

### Inferences
- A usable, attributable channel framework is Thompson's "primary taste + sequence": name the lead taste of each dish, then the supporting ones. For pad kra pao, from the sources above: lead = salty (fish sauce) + hot; aroma = holy basil and fried garlic; support = a little sweet; sour = none in the pan, only from the condiment. This is my synthesis; no single source states it as a rule.
- "กลมกล่อม" should be explained as "rounded — no single taste sticks out", not "all four tastes equal"; none of the recipes read is anywhere near equal parts.
- "Old-style" needs a caption each time: (a) 1950s cook-shop style = black soy/bean sauce + fish sauce, mild; (b) 1954–1976 printed home recipes = fish sauce (± salt, sugar, MSG), no soy or oyster sauce. The fish-sauce-only "purist" version is (b), not the oldest known practice.

### Gaps
- No Thai culinary-school textbook or Royal Institute definition of กลมกล่อม / รสชาติ was found; the framework rests on Thompson (non-Thai, though a recognised authority) and a Thailand Foundation article.
- No Thai source read states a sweetness limit in words (e.g. "must not taste sweet"); it is implied by quantities only.
- Thompson's "sweet, sour, hot, salty… tasting, tasting" quote is snippet-level; needs the page opened before quoting.

## 2. Technique, step by step, with the reason for each step

### Takeaway
The steps are consistent across authorities — coarse-pounded chilli and garlic, hot fat, quick high-heat cooking, basil last and only until wilted — but written *reasons* are thin: Pailin gives the most explicit ones (browning for flavour, basil off heat), Krit gives timing logic, and no food-science paper measuring holy-basil aroma loss during stir-frying was found.

### Cited Findings
**Step 1 — Pound, do not purée**
- [P, browser] Krit: bird's-eye chillies, Thai garlic and a little pepper "ตำรวมกันพอหยาบๆ" (pounded together just coarsely). In the retro recipe: yellow chi fa with a little red chilli pounded with garlic "จนเกือบๆ ละเอียด". — [Matichon 2018](https://www.matichon.co.th/lifestyle/news_827757); [Matichon 2019](https://www.matichon.co.th/lifestyle/news_1827717)
- [P, browser] Pailin: pound the Thai chillies "into a fine paste" first, then garlic and mild chillies "just into a rough paste… until there are no more big chunks". Mortar and pestle is listed as the recipe's equipment. — [Hot Thai Kitchen, beef](https://hot-thai-kitchen.com/pad-kra-pao-beef/); [Hot Thai Kitchen, anything](https://hot-thai-kitchen.com/pad-kra-pao-anything/)
- [S] M.L. Tiw 1954: pound garlic with salt and pepper; bruise the chillies and basil flowers. — [Silpa-Mag](https://www.silpa-mag.com/culture/article_148492)
- [U] J. Kenji López-Alt's recipe note: a mortar and pestle "will produce the most flavorful results, but you can easily make the dish without one". Snippet of a reprint; original page blocked. — [Serious Eats](https://www.seriouseats.com/thai-style-beef-with-basil-and-chiles-recipe)

**Step 2 — Fat and heat**
- [P, browser] Krit: heat the oil until hot, then "พยายามรีบผัดด้วยไฟแรง" (stir-fry fast over high heat). Retro recipe: lard, over "ไฟกลางค่อนข้างแรง" (medium-high), fry the paste "ให้สีเหลืองสวย กลิ่นหอมโชยขึ้นมา" (to a good yellow, until the aroma rises) before the meat goes in. — [Matichon 2018](https://www.matichon.co.th/lifestyle/news_827757); [Matichon 2019](https://www.matichon.co.th/lifestyle/news_1827717)
- [S] Fat quantities: M.L. Tiw ¼ cup lard to 2 cups beef; Thompson 3–4 tbsp oil to 200 g beef; Leela 2 tbsp to 1 lb; Pailin 2 tbsp to 12 oz (1 tbsp only "if your beef is lean"). — sources as in `ingredient_cards_gapfill.md` §6; [Hot Thai Kitchen, beef](https://hot-thai-kitchen.com/pad-kra-pao-beef/)

**Step 3 — Order of meat and aromatics (two schools)**
- [P, browser] Aromatics first (Krit retro, M.L. Tiw, Thompson): fry paste, then meat, then fish sauce. — [Matichon 2019](https://www.matichon.co.th/lifestyle/news_1827717)
- [P, browser] Meat first (Pailin): on a very hot wok spread the beef and "Sear the beef without moving it until the underside is browned"; keep going until the released liquid "has evaporated, and the beef starts to sizzle in its own fat", then brown again in 15–20 second rests "to brown the beef further and develop flavour". Then turn down to medium, push meat aside, fry the paste about 30 seconds "until aromatic and the garlic starts to turn golden". — [Hot Thai Kitchen, beef](https://hot-thai-kitchen.com/pad-kra-pao-beef/)
- [P, browser] Pailin's limit on that method: with chicken or pork "skip the prolonged browning technique… because chicken and pork can become too dry". — [Hot Thai Kitchen, beef](https://hot-thai-kitchen.com/pad-kra-pao-beef/)
- [P, browser] Krit, for quick-cooking seafood: paste and seafood go into the hot oil together so nothing overcooks. — [Matichon 2018](https://www.matichon.co.th/lifestyle/news_827757)

**Step 4 — Seasoning and liquid**
- [P, browser] Pailin modern: pre-mix the sauce and "Stir until the sugar is completely dissolved"; add with the protein on high heat and toss "just for 30 seconds or so". Old-school: add about half the stock with fish sauce and sugar; more only if dry. — [Hot Thai Kitchen, anything](https://hot-thai-kitchen.com/pad-kra-pao-anything/); [Hot Thai Kitchen, beef](https://hot-thai-kitchen.com/pad-kra-pao-beef/)
- [P, browser] Krit: "ขยอกน้ำปลาใส่" (a glug of fish sauce) straight into the pan; no water. — [Matichon 2018](https://www.matichon.co.th/lifestyle/news_827757)

**Step 5 — Basil last, briefly**
- [P, browser] Krit: add the basil at the end and time it "ให้ใบกะเพราสลดในเวลาเดียวกับที่ของ… สุกในจานพอดี" (so the leaves wilt just as the food finishes cooking on the plate). Retro: add basil when the meat turns pink, "ผัดเร็วๆ". — [Matichon 2018](https://www.matichon.co.th/lifestyle/news_827757); [Matichon 2019](https://www.matichon.co.th/lifestyle/news_1827717)
- [P, browser] Pailin: "Turn off the heat, add the basil and mix just until wilted." — [Hot Thai Kitchen, beef](https://hot-thai-kitchen.com/pad-kra-pao-beef/)
- [P, browser] Pailin on why aroma is fragile to begin with: holy basil's fragrance "is also milder than Thai basil, and unless it's freshly picked from a healthy plant, it can be rather faint". — [Hot Thai Kitchen, beef](https://hot-thai-kitchen.com/pad-kra-pao-beef/)
- [P, browser] Krit's booster: a pinch of sun-dried holy basil leaves added to the paste "หากต้องการกลิ่นฉุนหอมลึกๆ" (for a deeper pungent aroma). M.L. Tiw's is basil flowers bruised into the paste [S]. — [Matichon 2018](https://www.matichon.co.th/lifestyle/news_827757); [Silpa-Mag](https://www.silpa-mag.com/culture/article_148492)

**Food science on holy-basil aroma**
- [P, browser] Chulalongkorn thesis (Thanatcha Vichienkalayarat, 2022, sensomics/AEDA on fresh leaves): white holy basil's high-impact odorant is eugenol (FD ≥ 4096); red holy basil has eugenol (FD 2048) and methyl eugenol (FD ≥ 4096); red leaves scored stronger overall, more minty, green and pungent, less citrus-like. The Thai abstract says the SAFE extraction was used specifically "เพื่อลดการสูญเสียหรือการเปลี่ยนแปลงโครงสร้างของสารระเหยจากความร้อน" (to reduce loss or structural change of volatiles from heat) and that using fresh leaves in food products requires quality control through processing to keep the characteristic aroma. Upgrades the [U] entry in `ingredient_cards.md` §2. — [Chula DigiVerse, DOI 10.58837/CHULA.THE.2022.164](https://digiverse.chula.ac.th/Info/item/dc:36200)
- [P, fetch] Eugenol boils at about 252–256 °C at atmospheric pressure. — [PubChem CID 3314](https://pubchem.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/compound/3314)
- [P, fetch] A 2023 drying study on two Thai holy-basil cultivars (Wiset et al.) says in general terms that drying can cause "alterations in aroma caused by losses in volatiles", but its own results for methyl eugenol and β-caryophyllene across treatments are inconsistent and it has no fresh-leaf baseline. It does not support a quantitative "heat destroys X%" claim. — [Frontiers in Sustainable Food Systems 7, doi 10.3389/fsufs.2023.1219540](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fsufs.2023.1219540)
- [U] A study on sweet basil (not holy basil) reported that total volatiles fell considerably in oven- and freeze-drying but little in room-temperature air drying. Snippet. — [J. Sci. Food Agric., doi 10.1002/jsfa.1921](https://zendy.io/title/10.1002/jsfa.1921)

**Wok vs flat pan; wok hei**
- [P, browser] Pailin's instruction reads "Heat a wok or a large skillet over high heat" — she treats a skillet as acceptable. — [Hot Thai Kitchen, beef](https://hot-thai-kitchen.com/pad-kra-pao-beef/)
- [P, browser] Krit places the dish's origin in "ผัดไฟแรงๆ แบบร้านกุ๊กจีน" (high-heat stir-frying of Chinese cook shops). For the fried-rice variant he wants the rice to pick up "กลิ่นไหม้นิดๆ จากกระทะที่ร้อนจัด" (a slight scorched aroma from a very hot wok). He does not use the term wok hei, and he does not name this aroma as a requirement for the plain stir-fry. — [Matichon 2019](https://www.matichon.co.th/lifestyle/news_1827717)
- [U] General stir-fry guides: overcrowding lowers pan temperature and steams the food; home burners rarely reach restaurant heat. Non-Thai, snippet-level. — [The Woks of Life](https://thewoksoflife.com/how-to-make-stir-fry/); [America's Test Kitchen](https://www.americastestkitchen.com/lessons/articles/2352-what-is-wok-hei)

### Inferences
- Why pound: every authority pounds, but only Kenji (snippet) states a reason ("most flavorful"). The mechanism — crushing ruptures more cells than cutting and releases more aromatic oil — is general food knowledge that I did not source to a study on chilli/garlic; label it "the usual explanation" on screen, not a cited fact.
- Why basil last: the cooks' instruction is firm and consistent; the chemistry behind it is plausible but indirect. Eugenol's boiling point (~253 °C) is far above pan-sauce temperature, so "it boils off" is wrong; the defensible wording is "the aroma compounds are carried off with steam and time, so the leaves get the shortest cooking of any ingredient" — an inference, not a measured result.
- For a home stove abroad the sourced adaptation is Pailin's: very hot pan, meat spread out and left undisturbed to brown, liquid cooked off before the aromatics go in. This achieves browning without restaurant-level flame.
- Two legitimate orders exist (paste-first: Krit, M.L. Tiw, Thompson; meat-first: Pailin). The script should present paste-first as the older documented order and meat-first as a home-stove adaptation, attributed to Pailin.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed study found that measures holy-basil volatiles before and after stir-frying, or as a function of cooking time. The "add basil last" rule is cook-sourced only.
- No Thai-language source found that discusses wok hei (กลิ่นกระทะ) for pad kra pao by name.
- No source found on crowding the pan specific to this dish; batch size advice is from general stir-fry guides.
- Kenji López-Alt's mortar-vs-knife reasoning is unread (bot check).
- Pailin's video tutorials (said to contain "extra tips not mentioned in the blog post") were not watched.

## 3. Mistakes that make it taste "not Thai" — what is actually documented

### Takeaway
Only three "mistakes" are documented by named authorities with an effect on taste: the wrong basil, too many sauces masking the basil, and too-lean or over-browned white meat. Vegetables and oyster/soy sauce are disputed preferences, not errors (see matrix in `ingredient_cards_gapfill.md` §6); "wrong soy sauce" and "wrong fish sauce" are matters of degree according to Pailin.

### Cited Findings
- **Wrong basil.** [P, browser] Pailin: Thai basil's flavour "is so distinct from holy basil that you will end up with a dish that tastes quite different (though still tasty)"; Italian basil "is a closer flavour match". Holy basil is "less floral and more peppery" than Thai basil. [S] Leela: with sweet basil, call it pad bai horapha. [P, fetch] Michelin Guide: sweet basil lacks the original's peppery heat. — [Hot Thai Kitchen, beef](https://hot-thai-kitchen.com/pad-kra-pao-beef/); [SheSimmers](https://shesimmers.com/2012/05/pad-ka-prao-%e0%b8%9c%e0%b8%b1%e0%b8%94%e0%b8%81%e0%b8%b0%e0%b9%80%e0%b8%9e%e0%b8%a3%e0%b8%b2.html); [Michelin Guide TH](https://guide.michelin.com/th/th/article/features/phad-kaprao---how-the-japanese-have-embraced-the-thai-favourite)
- **Weak or wrong-type holy basil.** [P, browser] Krit: common market basil is large-leaved, soft and "เหม็นฉุน"; "ใส่มากๆ พาลจะไม่อร่อย" (using a lot of it makes the dish worse). Look for small thick green leaves with dark purplish young stalks, sold as mature sprigs at Isan vegetable stalls; pick a leaf and smell it first. — [Matichon 2018](https://www.matichon.co.th/lifestyle/news_827757)
- **Too many sauces / too much oil.** [P, browser] Krit, quoted in §1: sauces "จนหากลิ่นกะเพราไม่เจอ". — [Matichon 2018](https://www.matichon.co.th/lifestyle/news_827757)
- **Too much black soy.** [P, browser] Pailin: "if you put too much it tends to taste a bit bitter because of that molasses flavour"; its job is colour. — [Hot Thai Kitchen, soy sauce 101](https://hot-thai-kitchen.com/soy-sauce-101/)
- **Lean meat / overcooking.** [P, browser] Pailin: chicken — dark meat "so it will not be too dry"; pork — "not extra lean"; meat pieces "smaller than your average stir fry as pad kra pao is not good with big chunky pieces". — [Hot Thai Kitchen, beef](https://hot-thai-kitchen.com/pad-kra-pao-beef/); [Hot Thai Kitchen, anything](https://hot-thai-kitchen.com/pad-kra-pao-anything/)
- **Watery vegetables.** [P, browser] Pailin: red bell pepper as a chilli stand-in is "a bit too watery" (acceptable if nothing else). She herself adds onion or long beans. — [Hot Thai Kitchen, anything](https://hot-thai-kitchen.com/pad-kra-pao-anything/)
- **Japanese or Chinese soy instead of Thai.** [P, browser] Pailin: match the soy sauce to the cuisine, but "you can use the 'wrong' country's soy sauce and you will still have a fine-tasting dish, albeit slightly different"; Chinese dark soy "tends to be saltier" than Thai black soy, so hold back other salt. [S] Leela does not use Japanese-style soy in Thai cooking "unless in extreme cases". — [Hot Thai Kitchen, soy sauce 101](https://hot-thai-kitchen.com/soy-sauce-101/); [SheSimmers, soy sauces](https://shesimmers.com/2010/01/soy-sauces-used-in-thai-cooking-and-how.html)
- **Wrong fish sauce.** [P, browser] Pailin: Thai and Vietnamese fish sauce are interchangeable; the risk is low quality (diluted, with additives) and the unfiltered murky kind (mam nem / pla ra), which "is NOT what you want". In a fish-sauce-only recipe "it's important to use a good one". — [Hot Thai Kitchen, fish sauce 101](https://hot-thai-kitchen.com/fish-sauce-101/); [Hot Thai Kitchen, beef](https://hot-thai-kitchen.com/pad-kra-pao-beef/)
- **Additions abroad.** [P, fetch] Michelin Guide on Japan: heaps of coriander; even strawberry jam, which Thais find too strange. — [Michelin Guide TH](https://guide.michelin.com/th/th/article/features/phad-kaprao---how-the-japanese-have-embraced-the-thai-favourite)

### Inferences
- A fair on-screen ranking of what moves the taste away from Thai, by strength of sourcing: (1) basil type and freshness; (2) sauce load masking basil; (3) dry, lean meat; (4) black soy overdose (bitter). Everything else is "changes it slightly".
- "Too much sugar" and "too much oyster sauce" are not documented as errors by any source read; they follow only from Krit's "no sauce smell" target and from the small sugar quantities in the older recipes.

### Gaps
- No source found describing the taste effect of over-sweetening in this dish, or of crowding the pan.
- No side-by-side tasting of Thai vs Japanese soy sauce in a Thai stir-fry by a named authority.

## 4. Adjusting at the end

### Takeaway
The only dish-specific instruction found is Pailin's: taste after the basil goes in and correct with sugar or fish sauce; she also states flatly that too-salty cannot be fixed. No Thai-language authority was found describing end corrections for this dish.

### Cited Findings
- [P, browser] Pailin: after the basil, "Taste and adjust seasoning with more sugar or fish sauce as needed." — [Hot Thai Kitchen, beef](https://hot-thai-kitchen.com/pad-kra-pao-beef/)
- [P, browser] Pailin tests recipes with a saltier fish sauce (Squid, 1,670 mg sodium per tbsp) so readers using other brands do not "end up with something that's is too salty; which is something you cannot fix". Brand sodium per tbsp she lists: Megachef blue 1,500, brown 1,580; Thai Kitchen 1,360; Red Boat 1,490. — [Hot Thai Kitchen, fish sauce 101](https://hot-thai-kitchen.com/fish-sauce-101/)
- [P, browser] Too dry: add "a splash more water or stock". — [Hot Thai Kitchen, beef](https://hot-thai-kitchen.com/pad-kra-pao-beef/)
- [P, browser] Not fragrant enough: Krit's pre-emptive fixes are choosing pungent basil by smell and adding dried holy basil to the paste (§2). — [Matichon 2018](https://www.matichon.co.th/lifestyle/news_827757)
- [P, browser] At the table: prik nam pla is "a tableside seasoning booster" — the Thai way to add salt, heat and acid after cooking. — [Hot Thai Kitchen, beef](https://hot-thai-kitchen.com/pad-kra-pao-beef/)
- [U] Generic (non-Thai) advice for over-salted food: sweetness, acid, fat or more unseasoned bulk. Snippet. — [America's Test Kitchen](https://www.americastestkitchen.com/articles/5816-is-your-dish-too-salty-sweet-sour-spicy-heres-how-to-fix-it)

### Inferences
- Script rule supported by Pailin: season low, taste, then add — because salt can go up but not down. For a too-salty pan the only sourced moves are a splash of stock (dilutes, makes it saucier) and serving with more plain rice; present these as practical, not traditional.
- Under-salting in the pan and finishing with prik nam pla at the table is consistent with how the sources serve the dish.

### Gaps
- How Thai cooks correct "too sweet" in a stir-fry — nothing found.
- No Thai-language chef or teacher source found on end-of-cooking correction for pad kra pao.

## 5. Seasonings pillar gaps

### Takeaway
Partly closed. Fish sauce: Chonburi (first factory, Tiparos) and Samut Songkhram (Squid) are confirmed from official or semi-official pages; a full "main provinces" list still rests on a non-official sector report. Label reading: Thai law's แท้/ผสม types (already in the earlier note) plus Pailin's ingredient-list and protein rules; "°N" is a Vietnamese/export convention, not Thai law. Oyster sauce: still no Thai standard found. Soy sauce: a TIS (มอก. 252) with four types is reported, but only at snippet level.

### Cited Findings
**Fish sauce — places and fish**
- [P, fetch] Thailand Foundation (5 Sep 2022): "The first-ever fish sauce factory was then established in Chonburi"; Tiparos is described as the first Thai fish-sauce manufacturer; "Thai fish sauce is usually made from anchovies"; commercial fermentation "three months or up to two years"; re-fermenting the residue in brine downgrades quality. — [Thailand Foundation](https://thailandfoundation.or.th/?p=512794)
- [P, fetch] US FDA warning letter, 12 Dec 2017: Thai Fishsauce Factory Co., Ltd. (Squid brand fish sauce), Muang District, Samut Songkhram Province. — [US FDA](https://www.fda.gov/inspections-compliance-enforcement-and-criminal-investigations/warning-letters/thai-fishsauce-factory-co-ltd-540119-12122017)
- [U] A 2026 sector report (Brandmine) says producers cluster on a roughly 100 km Gulf-coast arc from Samut Prakan through Chonburi to Rayong; names Tiparos/Tang Sang Hah (Chonburi), Rayong Fish Sauce Industry (Rayong), Deksomboon (Samut Sakhon). Internal date conflicts noted (Tang Sang Hah 1914 vs 1919). Snippet; not official. — [Brandmine sector report](https://wcfhbzbmxztdzwjaujoq.supabase.co/storage/v1/object/public/insights/sector-spotlight/2026-05-13-thailand-sauce-dynasties/thailand-sauce-dynasties-en.pdf)
- [U] A Thai agricultural-journal article: anchovy (ปลากะตัก) abundance in the south-west monsoon is highest in the eastern Gulf from Chonburi to Trat. Snippet. — [TCI-ThaiJO](https://li01.tci-thaijo.org/index.php/agritechjournal/article/download/269965/182789/1178365)
- [P/U] Species: JIRCAS names *Stolephorus indicus*, *S. commersonii* (ไส้ตัน) and *Clupeoides* sp. (กะตัก) [P, earlier note]; Dept. of Fisheries: Gulf anchovies fall in *Encrasicholina* and *Stolephorus* [U, earlier note]. — [JIRCAS](https://www.jircas.go.jp/en/database/thaifermented/nam-plaa); [Dept. of Fisheries](https://www4.fisheries.go.th/local/file_announce/202406261056331_announce.pdf)

**Fish sauce — reading a label**
- [P, earlier note] Thai law (MOPH Notification 203): น้ำปลาแท้ needs total nitrogen ≥ 9 g/L; น้ำปลาผสม ≥ 4 g/L; both ≥ 200 g/L salt; the type name must appear on the label. — [Thai FDA Compi_203](https://exfood.fda.moph.go.th/law/data/complilation/Compi_203.pdf)
- [P, browser] Pailin's three checks: (1) ingredient list no longer than anchovies (or "anchovy extract"), water, salt, sugar — MSG, flavour or colour signal dilution; (2) "Choose fish sauce with at least 2 grams of protein per tablespoon" (Red Boat 4 g; Squid and Megachef 2 g; one cheap brand 0 g); (3) not the cheapest, compared within one shop. — [Hot Thai Kitchen, fish sauce 101](https://hot-thai-kitchen.com/fish-sauce-101/)
- [P, browser] "°N": some brands print it (Red Boat 40°N, Megachef 30°N); higher means more protein. Megachef's blue label is export-only, brown is the domestic formula. — [Hot Thai Kitchen, fish sauce 101](https://hot-thai-kitchen.com/fish-sauce-101/)
- [P, browser] Process behind the quality gap: first-press liquid after 12–24 months; makers then add water and salt for further pressings and blend; "Most brands will also add a little bit of sugar". — [Hot Thai Kitchen, fish sauce 101](https://hot-thai-kitchen.com/fish-sauce-101/)
- [U] A Department of Medical Sciences survey of 471 samples reportedly found 62.8% of "genuine" and 37.1% of "mixed" fish sauces met Notification 203. Snippet, date not seen. — [MGR Online](https://mgronline.com/qol/detail/9560000055817)

**Oyster sauce**
- [P, browser] Pailin: Chinese in origin (Lee Kum Sheung, 1888, as told by Lee Kum Kee); made from "oyster extract" (oyster cooking liquid), thickened with starch, with salt, sugar, soy sauce, MSG and colour added; "Brands vary significantly"; she was "so surprised at how different they taste; especially when comparing the Thai and Chinese versions". Her ranking: LKK Premium, then Maekrua and Panda tied, then Healthy Boy. — [Hot Thai Kitchen, oyster sauce 101](https://hot-thai-kitchen.com/oyster-sauce-101/)
- [P, browser] Label reading: price and protein per serving are the useful guides; ingredient-list position of "oyster extract" is not reliable across brands (Maekrua lists it first, Panda fourth, yet she ranked them equal). Maekrua is "the most popular Thai brand". — [Hot Thai Kitchen, oyster sauce 101](https://hot-thai-kitchen.com/oyster-sauce-101/)
- [U] America's Test Kitchen described Megachef as a Thai-style oyster sauce "slightly sweeter"; a cooking site says Thai oyster sauce is usually lighter and sweeter than Chinese. Snippets. — [America's Test Kitchen](https://www.americastestkitchen.com/taste_tests/2792-all-about-oyster-sauce); [The Woks of Life](https://thewoksoflife.com/thai-soy-sauce/)
- [U] A Thai journal article: oyster sauce was the 5th best-selling product in the sauces-and-seasonings group in 2020 (8.3%). Snippet. — [TCI-ThaiJO](https://li01.tci-thaijo.org/index.php/tstj/article/download/251540/173445)

**Soy sauces — ซีอิ๊วขาว / ดำเค็ม / ดำหวาน and equivalents abroad**
- [U] TIS มอก. 252-2521 reportedly defines ซีอิ๊ว as liquid from fermenting soybean protein, flavoured/coloured or not, pasteurised, in four types (ซีอิ๊วขาว, ซีอิ๊วเค็ม/ดำเค็ม, ซีอิ๊วดำ, ซีอิ๊วหวาน), each in two grades; protein minimums read from a garbled table as roughly 5.5/4.5% (white), 8.5/7.5% (black), 2.0/1.5% (sweet). Snippet of a thesis chapter; numbers unreliable; standard is from 1978 and may be superseded. — [Chula thesis ch. 2](https://cuir.car.chula.ac.th/dspace/bitstream/123456789/70808/5/Sudawadee_le_ch2_p.pdf)
- [P, browser] Pailin's functional grouping (she states it is her own system, "not an official categorization"): regular soy (Thai "thin soy sauce", ซีอิ๊วขาว) = salt, umami, soy flavour; black/dark soy (ซีอิ๊วดำ) = colour and "a deeper, molasses-y flavour"; sweet soy = sweetness, "in Thailand we use it very rarely, usually to make dipping sauces"; seasoning sauces (Golden Mountain, Maggi) = comparable saltiness, different flavour. — [Hot Thai Kitchen, soy sauce 101](https://hot-thai-kitchen.com/soy-sauce-101/)
- [P, browser] Thai black soy comes in many formulas ("Some are darker, some are lighter, some are sweeter, some are saltier"); she prefers Dragonfly (orange lid), then Healthy Boy formula 1, which is "MUCH darker". — [Hot Thai Kitchen, soy sauce 101](https://hot-thai-kitchen.com/soy-sauce-101/)
- [P, browser] Equivalents abroad per Pailin: Thai thin soy → any bottle labelled "soy sauce" or "light soy sauce" (Healthy Boy is the only Thai brand she has seen overseas); Thai black soy → Chinese dark soy, which is saltier; sweet soy → Indonesian ABC kecap manis. "Light" does not mean low-sodium. — [Hot Thai Kitchen, soy sauce 101](https://hot-thai-kitchen.com/soy-sauce-101/)
- [P, fetch] Michelin Guide: Thai/Chinese dark soy is syrup-thick, sweetened and coloured with molasses; Japanese/Korean-style light soy is thin and very salty. — [Michelin Guide TH](https://guide.michelin.com/th/th/article/features/phad-kaprao---how-the-japanese-have-embraced-the-thai-favourite)

**Sugar type**
- [S/P] Granulated white: Pailin (both recipes) [P], Thompson [S]. Palm sugar: the 1998 book ("น้ำตาลปี๊บ") [S, earlier note]; Leela's purist route ("a tiny bit of palm sugar") [S]. Unspecified "น้ำตาล": M.L. Tiw 1954 [S]. Krit's retro version is explicitly free of "น้ำตาลทราย" [P]. No source uses coconut sugar by name. — [Hot Thai Kitchen, beef](https://hot-thai-kitchen.com/pad-kra-pao-beef/); [Sanook Campus](https://www.sanook.com/campus/1415627/); [Matichon 2019](https://www.matichon.co.th/lifestyle/news_1827717)

**Seasoning sauce (ซอสปรุงรส) and MSG in older recipes**
- [P, browser] The 1976 จัดสำรับ ชุด 2 recipe recommends MSG: "ที่จริงในเล่มแนะให้ใส่ผงชูรส แต่ผมไม่ใส่" (Krit). Confirms the [S] Sanook report in `ingredient_cards.md` §1 that 1970s recipes used fish sauce plus MSG. — [Matichon 2019](https://www.matichon.co.th/lifestyle/news_1827717)
- [P, browser] Krit counts ซอสปรุงรส among the modern additions absent from the old recipe. Pailin: seasoning sauces are "rarely 'core' to a dish's flavour". [S] Leela treats thin soy and seasoning sauce as interchangeable in her recipe. — [Matichon 2019](https://www.matichon.co.th/lifestyle/news_1827717); [Hot Thai Kitchen, soy sauce 101](https://hot-thai-kitchen.com/soy-sauce-101/)
- [P, earlier note] Thai law groups ซีอิ๊ว and ซอสปรุงรส in one notification (soybean-protein seasoning products, 2010). — [Thai FDA Compi_317](https://exfood.fda.moph.go.th/law/data/complilation/Compi_317.pdf)

### Inferences
- Safe on-screen geography: "Thailand's fish-sauce industry grew up on the upper Gulf coast — the first factory was in Chonburi, and a major brand is made in Samut Songkhram." Do not show a ranked list of provinces or production shares; none is sourced.
- Avoid "nitrogen grades" for Thai bottles. Thai law gives two label words (แท้ / ผสม) with minimums; °N is printed only on some export bottles. Abroad, the practical test is Pailin's protein-per-tablespoon check on the nutrition panel.
- For oyster sauce the honest line is: "no Thai standard found; brands differ a lot; Thai ones are generally reported as a little sweeter" — the last part is [U].
- ซีอิ๊วดำหวาน (Krit's 1950s cook-shop seasoning) and the ซีอิ๊วดำ in modern recipes are different products; kecap manis is the closest export equivalent of the former, Chinese dark soy (used sparingly) of the latter.

## 6. Serving — rice, fried egg, prik nam pla

### Takeaway
The plate is described consistently: over hot jasmine rice, a crisp-edged fried egg, and prik nam pla. Stated functions: the rice carries the pan juices, the condiment seasons the egg (Pailin), and the prik nam pla is where lime sourness enters. No source explains the origin of the egg pairing.

### Cited Findings
- [P, browser] Krit: served over "ข้าวสวยร้อนๆ หุงเป็นตัว" (hot rice cooked to separate grains), "เหยาะน้ำปลาพริกขี้หนูมะนาวสักนิด" (a small splash of fish sauce with chilli and lime), and "ไข่เป็ดดาวกรอบๆ" (a crisp fried duck egg). — [Matichon 2018](https://www.matichon.co.th/lifestyle/news_827757)
- [P, browser] Krit, fried-rice variant: firm-cooked ข้าวเสาไห้ stays in separate grains; the rice "ซึมซับน้ำ เนื้อ และน้ำมัน" (soaks up the juices and fat); with raw long beans and prik nam pla "ถึงไร้ไข่ดาวก็ไม่รู้สึกอะไร" (you don't miss the egg). — [Matichon 2019](https://www.matichon.co.th/lifestyle/news_1827717)
- [P, browser] Pailin: "served on rice, and usually with a fried egg on top"; eggs "Technically optional, but highly recommended". — [Hot Thai Kitchen, beef](https://hot-thai-kitchen.com/pad-kra-pao-beef/)
- [P, browser] Pailin on the condiment's job: "Pad kra pao is usually served with it because the fried egg can always use some extra seasoning"; "I like to put it on my fried egg to season it, but some people put it on their rice." — [Hot Thai Kitchen, beef](https://hot-thai-kitchen.com/pad-kra-pao-beef/)
- [P, browser] Egg method (Pailin): about ¼ inch of oil, medium-high; fry until the white's edges brown; yolk runny. [S] Thompson: "crispy, frazzled edges". — [Hot Thai Kitchen, beef](https://hot-thai-kitchen.com/pad-kra-pao-beef/); [Gourmet Traveller](https://www.gourmettraveller.com.au/recipe/chefs-recipes/david-thompson-stir-fried-minced-beef-with-chillies-and-holy-basil-neua-pat-bai-grapao-7362/)
- [P, browser] Prik nam pla in this recipe: 2 tbsp fish sauce, 1–2 chopped Thai chillies, 2 tsp lime juice, optional garlic; lime is optional and to taste ("some people don't even add any"). — [Hot Thai Kitchen, beef](https://hot-thai-kitchen.com/pad-kra-pao-beef/)
- [P, fetch] Thailand Foundation calls prik nam pla sour, salty, sweet and spicy. — [Thailand Foundation](https://thailandfoundation.or.th/?p=512794)
- [P, browser] In Thailand Maggi seasoning is "commonly drizzled over fried eggs" (Pailin) — an alternative egg seasoning. — [Hot Thai Kitchen, soy sauce 101](https://hot-thai-kitchen.com/soy-sauce-101/)
- [U] A Thairath poll reportedly found 61.24% prefer the egg with crisp edges and runny yolk. Snippet. — [Thairath](https://www.thairath.co.th/lifestyle/food/2878849)

### Inferences
- The plate, not the pan, is the balanced unit: salty-hot-aromatic stir-fry + plain rice (dilutes salt and heat) + rich egg yolk + salty-sour-hot condiment. This matches Thompson's point (snippet) that balance works across components, but no source says it of this dish specifically.
- "The egg cools the heat" is a common claim with no source found; do not state it as fact.

### Gaps
- Origin and dating of the fried-egg pairing — nothing found.
- No source found on why jasmine rice specifically (vs other rice) suits the dish, beyond Krit's remark on grain texture.
