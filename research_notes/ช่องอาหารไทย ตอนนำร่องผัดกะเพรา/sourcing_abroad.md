## 1. Import rules for individuals receiving plant products and sauces by post/courier (US, UK, EU, Australia, Canada, Japan)

### Takeaway
In every market where a rule could be confirmed, fresh herbs/chillies and seeds for planting sent from Thailand to a private person are regulated plant material that needs at least a Thai-government phytosanitary certificate (EU, Japan, UK for most fruit/veg, US for seeds), and Australia lists planting seeds among items that should not be mailed. Shelf-stable bottled sauces and milled rice are the low-friction category, but they sit under separate food/animal-product personal-import rules (e.g. 20 kg fish-product allowance in EU/GB, 20 kg/20 L condiments in Canada), not under an exemption that was confirmed for every country.

### Cited Findings

**United States (USDA APHIS)**
- [P] Page "Shipping Plants, Food, and Other Agricultural Items via Express Courier", last modified 2026-01-25: fresh fruits and vegetables, and "dried and processed plant products (e.g., rice, herbs, citrus peel, handicrafts)" are listed as items that "may be restricted or prohibited"; restrictions depend on country of origin. — [APHIS](https://www.aphis.usda.gov/plant-imports/shipping-plants-food-other-agricultural-products-express-courier)
- [P] Same page: seeds for planting generally "must obtain a phytosanitary certificate for each shipment and an import permit issued by APHIS"; the package's intended recipient is treated as the importer of record; packages may be seized and destroyed at the port of entry. — [APHIS](https://www.aphis.usda.gov/plant-imports/shipping-plants-food-other-agricultural-products-express-courier)
- [P-excerpt] APHIS publications state fresh fruit and vegetables "often cannot be imported directly by individuals for personal consumption", and that where a seller will not provide a phytosanitary certificate for seed, a Small Lots of Seed permit is the alternative. — [APHIS mail leaflet](https://www.aphis.usda.gov/publications/plant_health/PPQ-packages-thru-mail.pdf); [APHIS checklist](https://www.aphis.usda.gov/sites/default/files/checklist-shipping-agricultural-items.pdf)
- [P-excerpt] Legal basis of small lots of seed: final rule published 2006-04-13, effective 2006-05-15 (71 FR 19097), allowing small lots under an import permit instead of a phytosanitary certificate. — [Federal Register via govinfo](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2006-04-13/pdf/06-3554.pdf)
- [S] Limits described by an Australian government notice about the US programme: up to 50 packets per lot, each packet not more than 50 seeds of one taxon. — [DAFF Industry Advice Notice 2006/20](https://www.agriculture.gov.au/export/controlled-goods/plants-plant-products/ian/06/20)
- [P-excerpt] APHIS: small quantities of tomato and pepper seeds are NOT eligible under a PPQ 587 Small Lots of Seed permit; permit application via eFile can take up to 2 months. (Relevant: Thai chilli seed = Capsicum = pepper.) — [APHIS seeds with special requirements](https://www.aphis.usda.gov/plant-imports/buy-plants-seeds-online/seeds-additional-requirements)

**United Kingdom (GOV.UK / DEFRA-APHA) — Great Britain**
- [P] "You cannot bring in most fruit or vegetables unless you have a 'phytosanitary' (plant health) certificate for them" from countries outside the EU, Switzerland and Liechtenstein. — [GOV.UK fruit, vegetables, nuts, seeds](https://www.gov.uk/bringing-food-into-great-britain/fruit-vegetables-nuts-seeds)
- [P] Items listed as not needing the certificate include pineapple, kiwi, coconut, citrus, kumquat, persimmon, durian, curry leaves, banana/plantain, mango, dates, passion fruit, guava, "processed and packaged plant products, such as packaged salads or frozen plant material", peeled/processed nuts, and "certain grains, such as rice". Holy basil and chillies are not on the exempt list. — [GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/bringing-food-into-great-britain/fruit-vegetables-nuts-seeds)
- [P] Fish from non-EU countries: up to 20 kg per person in total of fish including fresh (gutted), processed (dried, cooked, cured or smoked) fish and fish products; honey and "other" items such as live mussels/oysters 2 kg; meat and milk products cannot be brought in. — [GOV.UK meat, dairy, fish](https://www.gov.uk/bringing-food-into-great-britain/meat-dairy-fish-animal-products)
- [P] Neither GOV.UK page states whether the traveller rules apply identically to parcels sent by post; the page is written for travellers and carries no date. — [GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/bringing-food-into-great-britain/fruit-vegetables-nuts-seeds)

**European Union (Regulation (EU) 2016/2031; Implementing Regulation (EU) 2019/2072)**
- [P] European Commission: from 14 December 2019 "all plants (including living parts of plants) will need to be accompanied by a phytosanitary certificate" to enter the EU unless exempted; exempt fruits are pineapples, coconuts, durians, bananas and dates. — [European Commission plant health rules](https://food.ec.europa.eu/plants/plant-health-and-biosecurity/plant-health-rules_en)
- [P] Same page: it is prohibited to bring plants, plant products and other objects in passengers' personal luggage "unless accompanied by a phytosanitary certificate" (examples: plants to be planted, fruits, vegetables, cut flowers). The page does not itself address postal parcels and does not mention seeds explicitly. — [European Commission](https://food.ec.europa.eu/plants/plant-health-and-biosecurity/plant-health-rules_en)
- [S] Croatian government guidance states the bans and restrictions also apply to small consignments sent to natural persons by post or courier. — [gov.hr](https://www.gov.hr/en/imports-of-plants-and-plant-products-in-passengers-per-sonal-luggage/1534)
- [S] Personal consignments of animal products (Delegated Regulation (EU) 2019/2122): fishery products (fresh eviscerated, prepared or processed) allowed up to a combined 20 kg or one fish; other animal products such as honey up to 2 kg from most third countries. — [Irish Dept of Agriculture summary](https://www.gov.ie/en/publication/aed28-personal-consignments-of-products-of-animal-origin/); [German customs, postal consignments](https://zoll.de/EN/Private-individuals/Postal_consignments_internet_order/Shipments-from-a-non-EU-country/Restrictions/Goods/Animals-and-plants-products-containing-animal-or-vegetable-substances/Protection-against-animal-diseases/protection-against-animal-diseases_node.html)
- [P-excerpt] A 2024 revision, Regulation (EU) 2024/3115, entered into force 5 January 2025; the Commission page still lists the same five exempt fruits. — [European Commission](https://food.ec.europa.eu/plants/plant-health-and-biosecurity/plant-health-rules_en)

**Australia (DAFF; BICON)**
- [P-excerpt] DAFF: "You can bring or mail some food, drinks and dietary supplements into Australia for personal use"; goods sent by mail must be accurately described on the postal declaration; anything not listed should be checked in BICON. — [DAFF bringing or mailing goods](https://www.agriculture.gov.au/biosecurity-trade/travelling/bringing-mailing-goods)
- [P-excerpt] DAFF's list of high-biosecurity-risk items that should not be sent to Australia includes "seeds for planting, such as flower, tree or vegetable seeds"; herbs and spices (including herbal teas) appear on the list of items that can be brought in. — [DAFF online sellers guide (PDF)](https://agriculture.gov.au/sites/default/files/documents/online-sellers-guide-english.pdf); [DAFF](https://www.agriculture.gov.au/biosecurity-trade/travelling/bringing-mailing-goods)
- [P-excerpt] Canned (retorted) bony fish and fish products may be imported for personal use, 10 kg or less, if they meet the BICON "Retorted goods" personal-use conditions. This is about canned goods, not fish sauce specifically. — [DAFF](https://www.agriculture.gov.au/biosecurity-trade/travelling/bringing-mailing-goods)
- [U] Third-party site: commercially packaged dried herbs/spices up to 1 kg allowed if declared; fresh herbs prohibited. Not confirmed on DAFF/BICON. — [canibringto.com](https://www.canibringto.com/can-i-bring/spices-to-australia)

**Canada (CFIA)**
- [P] Page modified 2026-05-26: "These requirements apply whether you are a traveller or are buying food online"; food must be for personal use and within CFIA maximum quantities. — [CFIA bringing food for personal use](https://inspection.canada.ca/en/food-safety-consumers/bring-food-personal-use)
- [P] Limits from countries other than the US: fresh vegetables 20 kg "if permitted" and "may be restricted or prohibited... depending on the country of origin" (check AIRS); processed fruit/vegetables incl. dried, frozen, canned (herbs fall here) 20 kg or 20 L; condiments and dressings 20 kg or 20 L with no product-specific restriction; fish and seafood 40 kg (dried fish 10 kg). — [CFIA](https://inspection.canada.ca/en/food-safety-consumers/bring-food-personal-use)
- [P] Personal imports are only allowed if there are no restrictions under other federal laws such as the Plant Protection Act; for nuts, grains, seeds and dried foods the page points to AIRS and says that if a document is required "the item won't be allowed into Canada without it". — [CFIA](https://inspection.canada.ca/en/food-safety-consumers/bring-food-personal-use)

**Japan (MAFF Plant Protection Station)**
- [P] Under the Plant Protection Act a phytosanitary certificate issued by the exporting country's government and import inspection are required "regardless of quantity, intended usage and so forth"; this covers personal baggage, mail and courier. Seeds, seedlings and bulbs are listed as items requiring import inspection. — [MAFF PPS English](https://www.maff.go.jp/pps/j/introduction/english.html)
- [P] Plants sent by mail must be in small packets/envelopes (not inside personal letters); some couriers refuse plants; inspection is free; Japan does not require an import permit for plants; processed products whose plant ingredients pose no pest threat are exempt (contact a Plant Protection Station for specifics); soil and certain country-specific fruit/vegetables are prohibited (check the Database for Importing Conditions). — [MAFF PPS English](https://www.maff.go.jp/pps/j/introduction/english.html)
- [P-excerpt] MAFF: plants arriving without a certificate are rejected and disposed of; some dried goods (cocoa beans, sesame seeds, dried tamarind, coriander, some dried flowers, herbal medicines and spices) still need a certificate; penalties up to 3 years' imprisonment or a fine up to 3 million yen. — [MAFF PPS quarantine info](https://www.maff.go.jp/pps/j/pqaqinfo_en.html)

### Inferences
- Holy basil seed (Ocimum tenuiflorum): no regulator page I opened names the species. By the general rules, a posted seed packet from Thailand would need a Thai phytosanitary certificate for the EU (as "plants", seeds not named explicitly on the Commission page) and Japan; for the US either a certificate plus permit or a Small Lots of Seed permit held by the buyer; Australia tells senders not to mail planting seed. This is inference from general rules, not a species-level confirmation.
- Chilli seed is a worse case than basil seed in the US because pepper seed is excluded from the small-lots route.
- The practical message for viewers is "do not order fresh kaprao, fresh chillies or seeds from Thailand by post; buy them inside your own country", while bottled sauces and rice are a different, generally permissive category that is far more easily bought locally anyway.
- UK exempt wording ("frozen plant material", "certain grains, such as rice") suggests frozen holy basil and rice are treated more leniently than fresh leaves, which fits with frozen holy basil being sold commercially in the UK (section 3).

### Gaps
- BICON case conditions for Ocimum tenuiflorum seed, fresh/dried herbs, fish sauce and oyster sauce were NOT retrieved (BICON is a JS database; DAFF mailing pages timed out). Australia's rule for sauces and for holy basil seed is unconfirmed.
- Canada: AIRS result for fresh herbs/chillies of Thai origin and for seeds for planting was not retrieved. Whether CFIA needs a phytosanitary certificate for small seed lots is unconfirmed.
- US: the current Small Lots of Seed page returned 404 at the URL tried; current packet limits and routing via a plant inspection station are confirmed only from a 2006 secondary notice. APHIS's ACIR database entry for fresh basil/chilli from Thailand was not checked. FDA rules (prior notice) for mailed sauces were not researched.
- UK: whether the traveller allowances apply to postal parcels, and the UK rule for seeds for planting from non-EU countries, were not confirmed from GOV.UK. Northern Ireland follows EU rules and was not checked separately.
- EU: whether oyster sauce (mollusc extract in a composite product) falls under the fishery allowance or the composite-product exemption was not confirmed; shelf-stable composite treatment not found. Annex XI of 2019/2072 as amended was not read.
- Japan: whether fresh holy basil or chillies from Thailand are on the prohibited list in the MAFF conditions database was not checked; Japan's food-sanitation rules for personally imported sauces were not researched.

## 2. Thailand's export side: phytosanitary certificates, Thailand Post / DHL, Shopee / Lazada cross-border

### Takeaway
I could not confirm from Thai primary sources how (or whether) a private person gets a Department of Agriculture phytosanitary certificate for a small consumer parcel, nor Thailand Post's own outbound food/plant list; these are open gaps. What was found suggests marketplace cross-border programmes are seller-side, invitation-based, and aimed at other Southeast Asian markets, not at consumers in the US/UK/EU/Australia/Canada/Japan.

### Cited Findings
- [S] DHL Thailand's restricted-items guide classes seeds (with antiques, medical devices) as "restricted", i.e. needing government permits or certificates rather than being banned outright. (Search excerpt; the page itself timed out.) — [DHL Thailand guide](https://www.dhl.com/discover/en-th/logistics-advice/essential-guides/restricted-items-shipping-guide)
- [S] A Department of Agriculture announcement (No. 2, B.E. 2558 / 2015) sets fee rates for sample analysis and issuing export certificates for agricultural goods; figures not read and may be outdated. — [DOA PSCO PDF](https://www.doa.go.th/psco/wp-content/uploads/2020/06/ประกาศ-กวก.-อัตราวิเคราะห์ตัวอย่างและออกใบรับรองสินค้าเพื่อส่งออก-ฉบับที่-2-2558.pdf)
- [S] Shopee International Platform (SIP): described as invitation-only; seller ships domestically and Shopee handles international leg; a 2023 Thailand piece lists Singapore, Philippines, Taiwan, Thailand, Chile; a June 2026 Philippine report says it supports cross-border selling among the Philippines, Malaysia and Thailand. None of these are Shopee's own Thailand terms. — [Bangkok Post](https://www.bangkokpost.com/business/2317042/shopee-unveils-shopee-international-platform-joins-forces-with-ditp-to-help-thai-smes-reach-consumers-around-the-region); [BigSeller](https://www.bigseller.pro/blog/articleDetails/231/What-is-Shopee-International-Platform-and-How-to-Join-it.htm?source=app); [InsiderPH](https://insiderph.com/shopee-helps-small-businesses-expand-across-southeast-asia)
- [S] Lazada: LazGlobal is for sellers based outside a market selling into it; Bangkok Post reported a plan to help Thai sellers export to ASEAN via a "Laz Go Global" feature (article date not shown). — [Bangkok Post](https://www.bangkokpost.com/business/2408028/lazada-chief-pursuing-sustainable-growth); [Duoke](https://www.duoke.com/en/blog/article/166-Choosing-Your-Lazada-Path-Benefits-and-Requirements-of-Marketplace-LazMall-LazGlobal)
- [P] Destination side confirms why this matters: Japan and the EU require the exporting government's certificate even for personal mail (see section 1). — [MAFF](https://www.maff.go.jp/pps/j/introduction/english.html); [European Commission](https://food.ec.europa.eu/plants/plant-health-and-biosecurity/plant-health-rules_en)

### Inferences
- A Shopee Thailand affiliate link is not a usable buying route for a viewer in the US/UK/EU/Australia/Canada/Japan: the evidence found shows no consumer-facing delivery from Shopee/Lazada Thailand to those markets. Treat as "not found", not "proven impossible".
- A truthful on-camera line is: "I can't ship fresh herbs or seeds to you, and you shouldn't order them from Thailand; here is how to get them where you live."

### Gaps
- Thailand Post's own outbound prohibited/restricted list for food, liquids (sauces) and plant items: not found. Searches returned only inbound rules or other posts' pages.
- Department of Agriculture phytosanitary certificate process for individuals (where to apply, inspection step, fee, lead time, e-Phyto): not retrieved. Whether it is realistic for a small parcel is therefore unanswered; no source was found saying it is routinely done for consumer parcels.
- DHL / FedEx Thailand rules on perishables and liquids: DHL page timed out; only the "seeds = restricted" excerpt is available.
- Shopee and Lazada Thailand official 2026 statements on international delivery to non-ASEAN buyers: not found.

## 4. Growing holy basil from seed in temperate climates

### Takeaway
Holy basil is sold as a seed-grown warm-season annual by mainstream seed companies in the US, UK, Australia and Sweden, so no import is needed; suppliers agree on shallow sowing with light, roughly 18-29 C for germination, and 5-20 days to emerge.

### Cited Findings
- [P-excerpt] Johnny's Selected Seeds (US): germination 5-10 days at 65-70 F (18-21 C); "Holy Basil requires some light for germination, so sow more shallowly than other basils"; plant 1/4 inch deep, keep at 70 F (21 C); 90-100 days to maturity. — [Johnny's](https://www.johnnyseeds.com/herbs/basil/holy-basil/holy-basil-seed-898.html)
- [S] Other US suppliers: soil 70-80 F, 7-14 days (Survival Garden Seeds); 70-85 F, 7-14 days, 60-90 days to maturity (Grow Organic / Strictly Medicinal); 68-70 F, 10-20 days (Kitazawa via True Leaf Market). — [Survival Garden Seeds](https://survivalgardenseeds.com/products/holy-basil-seeds-for-planting); [Grow Organic](https://www.groworganic.com/products/strictly-medicinal-organic-holy-basil-temperate); [True Leaf Market](https://www.trueleafmarket.com/a/amp/products/organic-basil-sacred)
- [S] US: Baker Creek (Rare Seeds) sells "Thai Holy Kaprao" basil, 100 seeds for USD 3.00, noting it grows best in warm summer months and struggles in cool conditions. — [Rare Seeds](https://www.rareseeds.com/collections/basil-seeds/products/basil-thai-holy-kaprao)
- [S] UK: Heritage Gardens sells "Basil Holy Thai" seed at GBP 2.00, sow indoors April to June. — [Heritage Gardens](https://www.heritagegardens.co.uk/products/basil-holy-thai)
- [S] Australia: The Seed Collection and Happy Valley Seeds sell holy basil / tulsi (Krishna, Rama, Kapoor "temperate", Amrita) and ship within Australia only. — [The Seed Collection](https://theseedcollection.com.au/holy-basil); [Happy Valley Seeds](https://www.happyvalleyseeds.com.au/products/tulsi-krishna)
- [S] EU (Sweden): Runabergs sells organic holy basil labelled Kra Pao / Tulsi, about 400 seeds per portion. — [Runabergs Froer](https://en.runabergsfroer.se/?p=531)
- [S] Mislabelling risk: a marketplace buyer reported a "holy basil" packet grew a non-basil weed; check the Latin name Ocimum tenuiflorum (syn. O. sanctum). — [Desertcart listing](https://www.desertcart.vn/products/237094080-holy-kaprao-basil-rama-tulsi-basil-ocimum-sanctum-packet-50)

### Inferences
- "Tulsi" strains sold for tea (Kapoor/temperate, Rama, Krishna, Amrita) are not necessarily the Thai culinary kaprao type; listings that say "Thai holy / kaprao" are the closer match. This distinction is drawn from the product names, not from a horticultural authority.
- In cool-summer climates it is a windowsill/greenhouse or high-summer crop.

### Gaps
- No RHS or university-extension source was retrieved; all growing data is from seed sellers.
- No in-country seed supplier identified for Canada or Japan.
- No source on yield, or on how many plants a household needs for regular pad kra pao.

- Thai tax treatment of foreign affiliate income: out of scope, not researched.

## 6. Disclaimers and disclosure requirements

### Takeaway
US and UK regulators both publish that affiliate links need an upfront, plain-language disclosure in the content itself, that platform tools alone are not guaranteed sufficient, and the FTC says US law applies to foreign creators whose content foreseeably reaches US consumers. TikTok's help pages require its commercial-content disclosure setting for promotional posts.

### Cited Findings
- [P] FTC "Endorsement Guides: What People Are Asking": suggested wording "I get commissions for purchases made through links in this post"; "paid link" next to the link is adequate; "affiliate link", "buy now" and "commissionable link" alone are not clear. — [FTC](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/ftcs-endorsement-guides-what-people-are-asking)
- [P] FTC: for video reviews with affiliate links, disclose "both in the videos and in the description near the links"; "The closer the disclosure is to your recommendation, the better." — [FTC](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/ftcs-endorsement-guides-what-people-are-asking)
- [P] FTC: a platform's disclosure feature "is no guarantee that it's an effective way" to disclose; if it is "reasonably foreseeable" that content will be seen by and affect US consumers, US law applies to a creator abroad; the UK and other countries have similar rules. — [FTC](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/ftcs-endorsement-guides-what-people-are-asking)
- [P] UK CAP AdviceOnline (22 Mar 2023): affiliate social posts must be "obviously identifiable as advertising upfront"; where all linked products are affiliate, include an identifier at the beginning, for example "Ad"; a standalone "affiliate" label, spaced-out "a d", "#collab", or a disclaimer at the bottom were found insufficient; "Both the business and the affiliate marketer are responsible under the Code". The page states it is not legal advice. — [ASA/CAP affiliate marketing](https://www.asa.org.uk/advice-online/affiliate-marketing.html)
- [P-excerpt] TikTok help: "When posting content that promotes a brand, product, or service on TikTok, you must turn on the Commercial Content Disclosure setting"; undisclosed commercial content may be ineligible for the For You feed. — [TikTok creator disclosure help](https://ads.tiktok.com/help/article/about-the-content-disclosure-setting-for-creators)
- [P] Regulator statements supporting a customs disclaimer: APHIS says the recipient is the importer of record and packages may be seized and destroyed; MAFF says a certificate is required regardless of quantity or purpose; CFIA says items may be restricted or prohibited depending on origin. — [APHIS](https://www.aphis.usda.gov/plant-imports/shipping-plants-food-other-agricultural-products-express-courier); [MAFF](https://www.maff.go.jp/pps/j/introduction/english.html); [CFIA](https://inspection.canada.ca/en/food-safety-consumers/bring-food-personal-use)
