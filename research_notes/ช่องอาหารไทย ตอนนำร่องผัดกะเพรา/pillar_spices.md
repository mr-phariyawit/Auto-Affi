## 1. Pepper (พริกไทย, Piper nigrum) in Thai cooking and in pad kra pao

### Takeaway
M.L. Tiw's 1954 recipe says only "พริกไทย 20 เม็ด" (20 peppercorns), pounded with garlic and salt; it does not say white or black, so the channel must not claim either colour as "the original". Chanthaburi pepper (พริกไทยจันท์) is confirmed in the DIP GI register as **สช 63100144** (DIP page dated 11 Aug 2563 / 2020), and the GI covers black AND white pepper, whole and ground, from two varieties grown in ten districts of Chanthaburi.

### Cited Findings

**Form of pepper in the oldest written pad kra pao recipe**
- [P] Silpa-Mag reproduces M.L. Tiw Chonlamakphichan's beef pad kra pao, broadcast 7 Feb 1954 (พ.ศ. 2497). Ingredient line: "พริกไทย 20 เม็ด" (pepper, 20 corns). Method: "โขลกพร้อมกับเกลือพริกไทยให้ละเอียด" (pound [garlic] finely with the salt and pepper). No colour word (ขาว/ดำ/ล่อน) appears; whole dried peppercorns are implied by "เม็ด" and by pounding. — [Silpa-Mag](https://www.silpa-mag.com/culture/article_148492)
- [P, from existing notes] Krit Lueamlamai: the early Chinese cook-shop version was "โรยพริกไทยป่นก่อนเสิร์ฟ" (dusted with ground pepper before serving); his own method pounds chillies, garlic and "a little pepper". Colour again unspecified. — [Matichon](https://www.matichon.co.th/lifestyle/news_827757)
- [S, from existing notes] Pailin's "old-school" pad kra pao lists ground pepper alongside fish sauce and sugar; colour not specified in the note. — [Hot Thai Kitchen](https://hot-thai-kitchen.com/pad-kra-pao-beef/)

**White vs black vs fresh green in Thai cooking**
- [P] The Chanthaburi GI notice defines the two dried forms. Black whole: from fully mature berries, threshed off the spike and dried with the skin on; "กลิ่นหอมฉุน รสชาติเผ็ดร้อน". White whole: from ripe mature berries with the skin removed, then dried; round smooth corns, cloudy white to brownish/greyish white, "ต้องเป็นสีธรรมชาติ ไม่ฟอกสีหรือย้อมแป้ง" (natural colour, not bleached or starch-dusted); "กลิ่นหอมฉุนแต่น้อยกว่าพริกไทยดำ และรสชาติเผ็ดร้อนกว่าพริกไทยดำแบบเม็ด" (less aromatic than black, but hotter than whole black). Both must weigh at least 500 g/litre. — [DIP public notice no. 150, book 46, 28 Apr 2563](https://webnew.ipthailand.go.th/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/gi_GI46.pdf)
- [P] Pailin Chongchitnant's massaman paste uses "½ tsp white peppercorns" (with coriander seed, cumin, cinnamon, cloves, nutmeg, cardamom). — [Hot Thai Kitchen](https://hot-thai-kitchen.com/massaman-curry-paste/)
- [P] A Rajamangala (RMUTP) home-economics lecture lists "พริกไทยดำ" (black pepper) among the dried spices that are toasted before use. — [RMUTP, ตอนที่ 9 เครื่องเทศและสมุนไพร](https://dk.rmutp.ac.th/upload/course/1774261337259443.pdf)
- [U] Thaitable: ground white pepper is used much more than ground black pepper in Thai cooking. — [thaitable.com](https://www.thaitable.com/ingredients/ground-pepper)
- [U] Leela Punyaratabandhu's garlic–peppercorn–coriander-root paste is described in search results as using whole white peppercorns (page returned HTTP 500, not read). — [She Simmers](https://shesimmers.com/2011/04/thai-garlic-peppercorn-cilantro-root.html)
- [U] Su-Mei Yu's "Big Four Paste" calls for Thai white peppercorns. — [Food Network](https://www.foodnetwork.com/recipes/the-big-four-paste-glurh-ka-tiem-prikk-thai-rugg-pakk-chee-recipe-1955637.amp)
- [U] Counter-example: an Asia Society "Thai Pepper-Coriander Paste" calls for whole black peppercorns. — [Asia Society](https://asiasociety.org/node/306)

**Growing provinces**
- [P] WIPO: Chanthaburi pepper has been grown in the province for "several hundred years"; the area "still produces most of Thailand's pepper"; cultivation is declining as farmers move to durian; Cambodian Kampot pepper is "harsh" competition. One farmers' association: 33 growers, about 21 ha (133 rai). — [WIPO IP Advantage, last update 14 Feb 2023](https://www.wipo.int/web/ip-advantage/w/stories/spotlight-on-thai-gis-thailand-s-treasured-delicacies)
- [P, abstract] Chulalongkorn thesis (1993) calls Chanthaburi Thailand's main pepper-growing area. — [Chula ETD 36836](https://digital.car.chula.ac.th/chulaetd/36836)
- [U] A Department of Agriculture research report says the main pepper sources are in the East (Chanthaburi, Trat, Rayong) with further areas in the South; the snippet says the underlying data are from 1998. — [DOA report 971.1](https://www.doa.go.th/plan/wp-content/uploads/2021/04/971.1ศึกษาและวิเคราะห์สภาพแปลงปลูกพริกไทยในแหล่งปลูกเพื่อลดต้นทุนการผลิต.pdf)

**Chanthaburi pepper GI (พริกไทยจันท์)**
- [P] DIP GI register page title: "144. สช 63100144 พริกไทยจันท์", page date 11.08.63 (11 Aug 2563 = 2020), with the registration document as a 3,590.72 KB download (not opened). — [DIP gi-011](https://www.ipthailand.go.th/th/gi-011/item/gi63100144.html); same on [DIP new site](https://webnew.ipthailand.go.th/gi/nzazoa-2020-08-11-000000/)
- [P] Public notice: book 46, notice no. 150, advertised 28 Apr 2563; application no. 62100206, filed 7 June 2562 (2019); applicant จังหวัดจันทบุรี. Name: "พริกไทยจันท์ (Chanthaburi Pepper และ/หรือ Prik Thai Chan)". Goods: (1) whole black, (2) whole white, (3) ground black, (4) ground white. Definition: dried whole and ground pepper "ที่มีรสชาติเผ็ดร้อน กลิ่นหอมฉุนเป็นเอกลักษณ์" from two varieties, "พันธุ์จันทบุรี (ปรางถี่)" and "พันธุ์คุชชิ่ง (ซาราวัคหรือมาเลเซีย)", grown and processed in 10 districts of Chanthaburi. Harvest December–April. — [DIP notice PDF](https://webnew.ipthailand.go.th/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/gi_GI46.pdf)
- [S] Suan Dusit University culinary school gives the registration date as 6 Aug 2563. — [food.dusit.ac.th, 4 Aug 2569](https://food.dusit.ac.th/home/?p=19021)
- [U] DIP licence report: "ครั้งที่ 759 พริกไทยจันท์ (ทะเบียนเลขที่ สช 63100144) จำนวน 11 ราย" dated 22.04.69. — [DIP gi-012](https://www.ipthailand.go.th/th/gi-012/item/gipersonlicensereport759.html)
- [S] Conflict: Bangkokbiznews describes the GI product as dried black pepper, whole and ground; the DIP notice lists black and white. Trust the DIP notice. — [Bangkokbiznews](https://www.bangkokbiznews.com/news/893514)
- [U] Other Thai pepper GIs exist: Trang pepper, application 63100262, registration สช 64100170, advertised in notice no. 176 dated 25 June 2564; a Satun (Su-ngai Upe) pepper GI document also appeared in results. Neither document was opened. — [DIP PDF](https://www.ipthailand.go.th/images/633/GI64100170_1.pdf); [DIP notice 176](https://ipthailand.go.th/images/3534/2564/GI/GIAN176-1.pdf)

**Piperine / aroma data**
- [P, abstract] Waraporn Putalun, M.Sc. Pharm. thesis, Chulalongkorn 1993 (DOI 10.58837/CHULA.THE.1993.859): two black pepper cultivars grown in Chanthaburi. Sri Lankan cultivar about 2.73% volatile oil and 4.96% piperine; Sarawak cultivar about 1.78% and 3.82% (significant at α=0.05). About 30 shared oil components; major ones α-pinene, β-pinene, sabinene, Δ³-carene, limonene, β-caryophyllene. Thai black pepper of either cultivar had more piperine but less volatile oil than samples from India, Brazil and Malaysia. Oil and piperine peaked at about 3 months' berry age; harvest recommended at about 5 months. — [Chula ETD](https://digital.car.chula.ac.th/chulaetd/36836)
- [U] A Thai thesis reported piperine about 1.78–2.60% w/w in black pepper from 5 planting areas in Chanthaburi. — [RSU repository](https://rsuir-library.rsu.ac.th/bitstream/123456789/426/1/Phitchapha%20Homa.pdf)
- [U] A 2022 HPLC-DAD study compared 15 Thai black pepper samples from different regions. — [PMC7879788](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7879788)

### Inferences
- Script-safe wording: "the 1954 recipe pounds whole peppercorns with garlic and salt; it does not say which colour." White is the more common ground pepper in Thai kitchens per several culinary writers, but that is [U]-grade evidence here.
- "More piperine, less volatile oil than Indian/Brazilian/Malaysian pepper" matches the Thai description "เผ็ดร้อน" but rests on one 1993 thesis; present it as "one Thai study found", not as settled fact.
- The thesis cultivar names (Sri Lankan, Sarawak) and the GI variety names (Chanthaburi/Prang Thi, Kuching/Sarawak) only partly overlap; do not equate "Sri Lankan" with "พันธุ์จันทบุรี" without a source.

### Gaps
- White vs black as "the traditional stir-fry pepper": no primary Thai culinary authority was read stating this. She Simmers (500) and David Thompson's books were not read.
- Fresh green peppercorns (พริกไทยอ่อน): no source opened this session. Their use in pad cha / jungle-curry-type dishes is common knowledge but unsourced here.
- The ten Chanthaburi districts are listed on notice pages 4–6, which I did not read.
- The GI registration certificate PDF was not opened, so the legal registration date (6 vs 11 Aug 2563) is not confirmed from the certificate.
- No current OAE area/yield statistics and no customs import figures for pepper were retrieved.
- No peer-reviewed white-vs-black aroma comparison specific to Thai pepper was found.

## 2. เครื่องเทศ vs สมุนไพร / ผักสวนครัว — how Thai sources draw the line

### Takeaway
Thai sources do not give one clean line. The dictionary sense of เครื่องเทศ is "pungent aromatic plant products, mostly from abroad"; a Thai home-economics lecture instead splits **เครื่องเทศสด** (fresh kitchen-garden aromatics whose oil fades on drying) from **เครื่องเทศแห้ง** (aromatic when dry, toasted before use). The fresh/dry split is the workable rule for the channel's pillars.

### Cited Findings
- [U] Royal Institute Dictionary B.E. 2554, as quoted in search results: เครื่องเทศ = "ของหอมฉุนและเผ็ดร้อนที่ได้มาจากพืช โดยมากมาจากต่างประเทศสำหรับใช้ทำยาไทยและปรุงอาหาร เช่น ลูกผักชี ยี่หร่า". The hosting page was not identified or opened; candidates in the result list were — [Chula Pharmacy e-learning](https://elearning.pharm.chula.ac.th/mod/resource/view.php?id=5836) and [Dailynews](https://www.dailynews.co.th/news/5514086/). Verify at the dictionary itself before quoting.
- [P] RMUTP lecture (อาจารย์วไลภรณ์ สุทธา, คณะเทคโนโลยีคหกรรมศาสตร์): "เครื่องเทศ (spice) หมายถึง ส่วนต่างๆ ของพืชที่ถูกนำมาใช้เพื่อปรุงอาหาร ให้สี กลิ่น และรสชาติ"; examples given are ginger, galangal, fingerroot. Distinctive traits: essential oil and pungency; sources are fruit, root, leaf, bark, seed, flower, bulb. — [RMUTP](https://dk.rmutp.ac.th/upload/course/1774261337259443.pdf)
- [P] Same lecture, two classes. **เครื่องเทศสด**: "พืชสวนครัวที่มีน้ำมันหอมระเหยในขณะที่ยังสดอยู่" whose aroma fades with drying: ผักชีฝรั่ง, โหระพา, แมงลัก, ขึ้นฉ่าย, กะเพรา, สะระแหน่, ขิง, ข่า, หอมแดง, ใบมะกรูด, ตะไคร้. **เครื่องเทศแห้ง**: aromatic when dry, more so when heated, "จึงนิยมนำไปคั่วก่อน": อบเชย, จันทน์เทศ, โป๊ยกั๊ก, กระวาน, พริกไทยดำ, ดีปลี, กานพลู, ยี่หร่า. — [RMUTP](https://dk.rmutp.ac.th/upload/course/1774261337259443.pdf)
- [P] Same lecture, by plant part: root กระชาย; rhizome ขิง ข่า ขมิ้น; bulb หอม กระเทียม; stem ตะไคร้; bark อบเชย; leaf กะเพรา แมงลัก โหระพา; flower ดอกจันทน์ กานพลู; fruit ลูกผักชี ยี่หร่า ลูกกระวาน โป๊ยกั๊ก พริก; seed พริกไทย. — [RMUTP](https://dk.rmutp.ac.th/upload/course/1774261337259443.pdf)
- [P] Same lecture on coriander: stem, root and seed (ลูกผักชี) are used "เป็นเครื่องเทศผสมเครื่องแกง"; the leaf is a garnish/side vegetable. On makrut: leaf and fruit peel flavour curry pastes. On fingerroot: fresh root used to mask fish odour, e.g. ขนมจีนน้ำยา. On turmeric: colours ข้าวหมกไก่, แกงเหลือง. — [RMUTP](https://dk.rmutp.ac.th/upload/course/1774261337259443.pdf)
- [U] A Thai news piece splits เครื่องเทศสด (โหระพา กะเพรา ขิง ข่า ตะไคร้) from เครื่องเทศแห้ง (พริกไทย อบเชย กานพลู ลูกผักชี). — [Dailynews](https://www.dailynews.co.th/news/4770793/)

### Inferences (proposed pillar assignment — an editorial rule, not a sourced fact)
- **Thai spices pillar** = RMUTP's เครื่องเทศแห้ง: sold and used dry, usually toasted/ground. Pepper, coriander seed, cumin, cardamom, cinnamon/cassia, cloves, star anise, nutmeg/mace, long pepper, makhwaen, dried chillies.
- **Thai vegetables/herbs pillar** = RMUTP's เครื่องเทศสด / พืชสวนครัว: galangal, lemongrass, makrut leaf and peel, coriander root, fingerroot, fresh turmeric, holy basil, fresh chillies.
- Borderline items need a stated rule on screen: dried chillies (same plant as fresh, but used dry and toasted → spices pillar); dried galangal and turmeric powder (dry form → spices pillar, fresh rhizome → vegetables pillar); coriander (root → vegetables, seed → spices).
- Calling lemongrass or galangal "สมุนไพร" is normal Thai usage but the RMUTP lecture files them under เครื่องเทศสด; the channel should say "fresh aromatics" in English to avoid the herb/spice argument.

### Gaps
- The Royal Institute definitions of เครื่องเทศ and สมุนไพร were not read at source.
- The lecture's definition of สมุนไพร (it is in the title) was not extracted; the text I read covered only the spice half.
- No historical cookbook (e.g. แม่ครัวหัวป่าก์) was consulted for period usage of the two words.

## 3. Master list of spices for the series

### Takeaway
Accepted names were checked in GBIF for every entry. Three need care on screen: Siam cardamom is now *Wurfbainia testacea* (syn. *Amomum testaceum*); makhwaen's usual label *Zanthoxylum limonella* is a synonym of *Z. rhetsa* in GBIF, while the Thai Department of Agriculture leaflet calls makhwaen *Z. myriacanthum*; and Thai ดีปลี is *Piper retrofractum*, not Indian *P. longum*. Only pepper has a confirmed GI among these.

**Uses, regions and GI — what was actually sourced**
- **Pepper**: see section 1. GI สช 63100144 (Chanthaburi) [P]; Trang and Satun pepper GIs [U].
- **Massaman paste spice set** [P]: Pailin's recipe uses toasted coriander seeds 2 tsp, toasted cumin seeds 2 tsp, white peppercorns ½ tsp, ground cinnamon 1½ tsp, ground cloves ½ tsp, ground nutmeg ¼ tsp, "ground green or white cardamom" ½ tsp, plus mild dried red chillies 40 g. In a comment she says unspecified "cardamom" usually means green. — [Hot Thai Kitchen](https://hot-thai-kitchen.com/massaman-curry-paste/)
- **Coriander seed** [P]: small round yellow-brown fruit; toast and grind before use; flavours curry pastes, pickles, sausage. — [RMUTP](https://dk.rmutp.ac.th/upload/course/1774261337259443.pdf)
- **Cinnamon/cassia** [P]: main compound cinnamaldehyde; used in เครื่องพะโล้ (five-spice braise) and baked goods; "มี 3 ชนิด คือ อบเชยญวน อบเชยเทศ และอบเชยจีน" (Vietnamese, "foreign"/Ceylon-type, Chinese) with different aromas. The lecture gives no Latin names. — [RMUTP](https://dk.rmutp.ac.th/upload/course/1774261337259443.pdf)
- **Cloves** [P]: dried flower buds, strong aroma, pungent; used to marinate meat and in stewed meat (เนื้อตุ๋น). — [RMUTP](https://dk.rmutp.ac.th/upload/course/1774261337259443.pdf)
- **Siam cardamom** [P]: JIRCAS lists *Amomum testaceum* Ridl., Thai "Krawaan", English "Best cardamom, Siam cardamom"; grows naturally under tall forest canopy above 500 m; at low altitude it rarely fruits and is grown for young shoots, which are cooked in chicken curries; fruit is the spice; harvest from year three. No provinces named. — [JIRCAS Thai vegetables database](https://www.jircas.go.jp/en/database/thaivege/007)
- **Siam cardamom** [U]: a 2568 Thai traditional-medicine journal article gives the current name as *Wurfbainia testacea* with *Amomum testaceum* as synonym and lists the local name กระวานจันท์; a Rambhai Barni Rajabhat University thesis says Chanthaburi growers plant กระวานจันท์ for its young shoots. — [JTTAM](https://he01.tci-thaijo.org/index.php/JTTAM/article/download/284841/189796/1356250); [RBRU thesis](https://eresearch.rbru.ac.th/pdf-uploads/thesis-2082-file05-2024-01-16-15-18-31.pdf)
- **Makhwaen** [P, partial]: the Department of Agriculture leaflet (Chiang Mai Royal Agricultural Research Centre) gives "ชื่อวิทยาศาสตร์: Zanthoxylum myriacanthum", family Rutaceae; a thorny tree 5–10 m; grows at about 200–1,000 m. I read only the first visible part of page 1. — [DOA leaflet](https://www.doa.go.th/hc/cmrarc/wp-content/uploads/2023/12/แผ่นพับมะแขว่น.pdf)
- **Makhwaen** [U]: a DOA research report says the Thai makhwaen group has several species including มะแขว่น (*Z. myriacanthum*) and is used mostly as a spice in ลาบ and แกงอ่อม in the North. — [DOA report 512/2562](https://info.doa.go.th/research/pdf/2562/512_2562.pdf)
- **Makhwaen** [S]: thaicookbook.tv calls it "a type of Prickly ash", "related to the Sichuan pepper", used most in Northern Thai cooking; no Latin name or substitute given. — [thaicookbook.tv](https://www.thaicookbook.tv/thai-food-ingredients/dry-herbs-and-spices/prickly-ash-seeds-thai-makhwaen)
- **Makhwaen GI**: a search of DIP material for มะแขว่น / น่าน returned no makhwaen GI. This is "not found", not "does not exist". — search only [U]
- **Long pepper** [S]: Thai Food Master files ดีปลี under *Piper retrofractum* (Javanese/Balinese long pepper), fruit "similar in appearance and taste" to Indian *P. longum*; in the Malay Archipelago the fruit was once called "cabai", a name later transferred to chilli. No Thai dishes listed on that page. — [Thai Food Master](https://thaifoodmaster.com/ingredient/javanese-long-pepper-piper-retrofractum)
- **Dried spices as a class** [P]: อบเชย, จันทน์เทศ, โป๊ยกั๊ก, กระวาน, พริกไทยดำ, ดีปลี, กานพลู, ยี่หร่า are named as the dried spices toasted before use. — [RMUTP](https://dk.rmutp.ac.th/upload/course/1774261337259443.pdf)
- **Name trap** [P]: the same lecture uses "ใบยี่หร่า" for a leafy herb in the basil family used in เนื้อผัดใบยี่หร่า and southern curries. So Thai "ยี่หร่า" can mean cumin seed or this leaf; scripts must say which. — [RMUTP](https://dk.rmutp.ac.th/upload/course/1774261337259443.pdf)

### Inferences
- For Siam cardamom the safest on-screen form is "*Wurfbainia testacea* (formerly *Amomum testaceum*)". Avoid "*Amomum krervanh*": databases disagree on it and it may refer to *W. vera*, a different taxon.
- For makhwaen, show "*Zanthoxylum* sp. — *Z. myriacanthum* per the Thai Department of Agriculture; often labelled *Z. limonella* (= *Z. rhetsa*)". Thai agronomic and commercial sources disagree, and I could not resolve which species is sold as makhwaen.
- Pailin accepting green cardamom in massaman is the sourced basis for the "can't find Thai white cardamom → use green" line.

### Gaps
- POWO was not consulted (bot check). GBIF could not resolve "Cinnamomum cassia"; the POWO-accepted name for Chinese cassia is unconfirmed here.
- The following dish associations are common knowledge but were NOT sourced this session and must be verified before scripting: cumin and coriander seed in red/green/panang/yellow pastes; star anise and cinnamon in phalo and boat-noodle broth; mace and nutmeg in massaman per older recipes; long pepper in northern laab spice mix (พริกลาบ) and southern curries; dried bird's-eye vs large dried chillies (พริกแห้งเม็ดใหญ่ / บางช้าง) by paste; dried galangal in laab spice; bay-type leaves in massaman.
- มะแหลบ: no source found linking the Thai name to a species or to dishes. "Ma-lab / prickly ash" in the brief may be a confusion with makhwaen; treat as open.
- ใบกระวาน: species identity in Thai trade (Indian bay *C. tamala* vs laurel vs another *Cinnamomum*) not sourced.
- Thai growing regions for anything other than pepper, Siam cardamom (habitat only) and makhwaen (altitude only): not sourced. No provinces should be named for makhwaen (e.g. Nan) or cardamom (e.g. Chanthaburi/Soi Dao) without a source; only the [U] RBRU thesis ties กระวานจันท์ to Chanthaburi.
- GI status of chillies (e.g. any dried-chilli GI), turmeric, garlic, shallot: not checked.

## 4. Native / Thai-produced versus imported

### Takeaway
Only pepper is firmly sourced as a Thai-grown spice (Chanthaburi and the East, with GI). Siam cardamom and makhwaen are sourced as plants growing in Thailand. For coriander seed, cumin, cinnamon, cloves, star anise and nutmeg, the evidence that Thailand imports them is snippet-grade only; no Thai agricultural or customs page was read.

### Cited Findings
- [P] Pepper: Chanthaburi "still produces most of Thailand's pepper". — [WIPO](https://www.wipo.int/web/ip-advantage/w/stories/spotlight-on-thai-gis-thailand-s-treasured-delicacies)
- [P] Chanthaburi GI varieties: local "จันทบุรี (ปรางถี่)" and introduced "คุชชิ่ง (ซาราวัคหรือมาเลเซีย)". — [DIP notice](https://webnew.ipthailand.go.th/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/gi_GI46.pdf)
- [P] Siam cardamom grows naturally in Thai forest above 500 m. — [JIRCAS](https://www.jircas.go.jp/en/database/thaivege/007)
- [P, partial] Makhwaen is described by the Department of Agriculture as a tree growing at 200–1,000 m and is the subject of a DOA Chiang Mai centre leaflet. — [DOA leaflet](https://www.doa.go.th/hc/cmrarc/wp-content/uploads/2023/12/แผ่นพับมะแขว่น.pdf)
- [U] Royal Institute Dictionary sense of เครื่องเทศ: "โดยมากมาจากต่างประเทศ" (mostly from abroad). Hosting page not opened; see section 2.
- [U] Trade-data aggregators in search results: Thailand's cumin seed imports (HS 090930) in 2023 dominated by India; cinnamon imports of about US$4.42 M from Indonesia in 2024; coriander imports from Italy, the US, Malaysia, Bulgaria, India, China and others. None opened; figures unverified. — [Tridge coriander](https://www.tridge.com/intelligences/coriander/TH/import); [Trading Economics](https://tradingeconomics.com/thailand/imports/indonesia/coffee-tea-mate-spices)
- [U] Thai growers report competition from Cambodian and Vietnamese pepper. — [Dailynews](https://www.dailynews.co.th/news/5732432/)

### Inferences
- The honest framing for the series: "pepper, Siam cardamom, makhwaen, long pepper and chillies are grown in Thailand; the 'Indian-route' spices of massaman (cumin, cloves, nutmeg, cinnamon) are traditionally imported." The second half is plausible and matches the dictionary sense, but is not yet backed by a primary Thai trade source.

### Gaps
- No Department of Agriculture, OAE or Customs Department page was read for production or import of any spice. This whole question needs a dedicated pass (Thai Customs HS 0904–0910 statistics; OAE pepper situation report).
- Long pepper and dried chilli production regions: not sourced.
- Whether Thai-market coriander seed and cinnamon are domestically grown at any scale: not sourced.

## 5. Availability abroad — import rules for dried spices and substitutes

### Takeaway
"Dried spices are fine" is true for the US and Canada in general terms, but it is NOT a safe blanket claim. Australia bans cumin, coriander and fennel seed and dried chillies in personal consignments unless khapra-beetle exclusions are met, and allows pepper only commercially packaged with a botanical-name label. Japan lists spice ingredients among items needing inspection. UK and EU pages read do not name dried spices explicitly.

### Cited Findings

**United States**
- [P] APHIS traveller page (last modified 13 Jan 2026): "Most dried spices can be imported", except "orange, lemon, lime, and other citrus leaves and seeds, and many vegetable and fruit seeds". APHIS "advise[s] against bringing lemongrass" unless commercially packaged, because it must be inspected for plant rusts. All agricultural products must be declared. — [APHIS](https://www.aphis.usda.gov/traveling-with-ag-products/coffee-tea-honey-nuts-spices)
- [P, from existing notes] APHIS ACIR search returns a separate document "Dried Pepper from Countries Free of Khapra Beetle" (Capsicum), i.e. dried chilli has its own conditions. Document text not read. — [ACIR](https://acir.aphis.usda.gov/s/acir-global-search?category=Plants-and-Plant-Products-Not-for-Propagation)

**Canada**
- [P] CFIA personal-use table (modified 2026-05-26): "Spices, tea, coffee" — restrictions "None", maximum 20 kg per person, from the US and from other countries. Processed fruit or vegetables "(including herbs) such as dried, frozen, canned" — restrictions "None", 20 kg or 20 L. Other dried foods: "Refer to the Automated Import Reference System." All food must be declared. — [CFIA](https://inspection.canada.ca/en/food-safety-consumers/bring-food-personal-use)

**Australia**
- [P] DAFF "Bringing or mailing goods": "Cumin, coriander and fennel seeds are not permitted and will be destroyed, unless they meet the exclusions listed on the urgent actions to protect against khapra beetle webpage." "Dried chillies/capsicum are not permitted and will be destroyed" on the same condition. "Some herbs and dried, ground spices and spice mixes may be allowed … A phytosanitary certificate may be required." — [DAFF](https://www.agriculture.gov.au/biosecurity-trade/travelling/bringing-mailing-goods)
- [P] DAFF on pepper: whole or ground pepper is allowed if "commercially prepared and packaged" and "labelled with the full botanical name of a permitted species"; it must be declared and may be inspected. — [DAFF](https://www.agriculture.gov.au/biosecurity-trade/travelling/bringing-mailing-goods)
- [P] DAFF on seeds: must be a permitted species, carry a phytosanitary certificate, and be commercially packaged and labelled with genus and species; "Certain seed species are hosts of khapra beetle and are banned from entry". — [DAFF](https://www.agriculture.gov.au/biosecurity-trade/travelling/bringing-mailing-goods)

**United Kingdom (Great Britain)**
- [P] From non-EU countries most fruit and vegetables need a phytosanitary certificate; exempt items include "processed and packaged plant products, such as packaged salads or frozen plant material" and curry leaves. Dried spices are not named; no weight limit is stated. — [GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/bringing-food-into-great-britain/fruit-vegetables-nuts-seeds)

**European Union**
- [P, from existing notes] All plants and living parts of plants need a phytosanitary certificate unless exempted (pineapple, coconut, durian, banana, dates). The page read does not address dried spices. — [European Commission](https://food.ec.europa.eu/plants/plant-health-and-biosecurity/plant-health-rules_en)
- The Commission's imports page returned HTTP 429 today; not read. — [European Commission imports page](https://food.ec.europa.eu/plants/plant-health-and-biosecurity/non-eu-trade/imports-eu_en)

**Japan**
- [P] MAFF Plant Protection Station: processed products whose plant ingredients pose no pest threat are exempt from inspection, but "Items used as ingredients for making spices, herbal medicines, etc." are listed among items requiring import inspection; travellers must present the exporting country's phytosanitary certificate, with exceptions for "certain plants" not listed on the page. — [MAFF PPS](https://www.maff.go.jp/pps/j/introduction/english.html)
- [P-excerpt, from existing notes] MAFF: some dried goods, including coriander and spices, still need a certificate. — [MAFF](https://www.maff.go.jp/pps/j/pqaqinfo_en.html)

**Hard-to-find Thai spices and substitutes**
- [P] Siam cardamom → green cardamom: Pailin's massaman recipe lists "ground green or white cardamom" as interchangeable. — [Hot Thai Kitchen](https://hot-thai-kitchen.com/massaman-curry-paste/)
- [S] Makhwaen is "related to the Sichuan pepper" — no substitute stated on the page. — [thaicookbook.tv](https://www.thaicookbook.tv/thai-food-ingredients/dry-herbs-and-spices/prickly-ash-seeds-thai-makhwaen)
- [S] Thai long pepper (*P. retrofractum*) is "similar in appearance and taste" to Indian long pepper (*P. longum*). — [Thai Food Master](https://thaifoodmaster.com/ingredient/javanese-long-pepper-piper-retrofractum)
- [U] Generic spice-blog pages suggest sansho or Sichuan pepper as stand-ins for one another; none addresses makhwaen specifically and none is an authority. — [search result example](https://spice.alibaba.com/spice-basics/sichuan-pepper-replacement)

### Inferences
- Whole coriander seed, cumin seed and dried chillies are exactly the items a Thai-spice kit would contain, and exactly the items Australia destroys. Any "we can ship a spice kit" claim must exclude Australia or be limited to ground, commercially packaged, botanically labelled product, checked against BICON first.
- Whole viable seeds (coriander, cumin) are the weak point everywhere: the US exception clause covers "many vegetable and fruit seeds"; Australia treats them as seed. Ground spice avoids the germination issue but not the khapra-beetle rules.
- For the US and Canada, "buy dried spices locally or bring them declared" is supportable from the regulator pages read.
- Substitution advice that can be stated with a source: green cardamom for Thai white cardamom (Pailin); Indian long pepper for Thai long pepper (similarity statement only, not an explicit substitution recommendation). Sichuan pepper for makhwaen is a reasonable suggestion but no credible authority was found recommending it.

### Gaps
- Australia: the khapra-beetle exclusions page and the BICON cases (dried herbs, powdered plant products, raw seed for human consumption, pepper) were not opened. Whether retail-packed ground coriander/cumin under 1 kg qualifies is OPEN.
- UK and EU: no regulator text read that names dried spices; whether they fall under "processed and packaged plant products" (UK) or outside the plant-health regime (EU) is not confirmed. Do not state "allowed" for UK/EU on current evidence.
- Japan: the list of exempt processed items was not found; the MAFF import-conditions database was not queried for pepper, coriander seed, cardamom or dried chilli.
- US: the ACIR documents for dried Capsicum and for dried/processed products were not read in full; khapra-beetle country status of Thailand not checked.
- Postal parcels vs traveller baggage: not distinguished on most pages read.
- Retail availability of Siam cardamom, makhwaen and Thai long pepper in each market: no retailer pages checked this session.
- No named culinary authority (Thompson, Punyaratabandhu, Chongchitnant) was found giving a makhwaen or long-pepper substitute.
