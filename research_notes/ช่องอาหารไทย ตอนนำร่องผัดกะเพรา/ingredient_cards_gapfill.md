## 1. Holy basil (Ocimum tenuiflorum) morphology for drawing; red vs white types

### Takeaway
A usable botanical description now exists from PROSEA (Plant Resources of South-East Asia): erect much-branched herb 30–60(–100) cm, woody at base, spreading hairs; opposite broadly elliptical leaves 1.5–6 × 1–2.5 cm on 0.5–2.5 cm petioles with a sparsely toothed, hairy margin; lax raceme 8–10 cm of whorl-like 3-flowered cymes; flowers 3 mm, pink or white. Red vs white is documented in a Thai journal as a stem-and-leaf colour difference (green-white vs green flushed purple-red), with red described as stronger-smelling. Kew POWO and Flora of China/Thailand treatments remain unread.

### Cited Findings
- [P*] Habit: "An aromatic, erect, much branched herb, 30-60(-100) cm tall with stem woody at base and an indumentum of patent hairs." — [PROSEA, O. tenuiflorum (author P.C.M. Jansen)](https://plantuse.plantnet.org/en/Ocimum_tenuiflorum_(PROSEA))
- [P*] Leaves: "decussately opposite; petiole 0.5-2.5 cm long; blade broadly elliptical, 1.5-6 cm × 1-2.5 cm", "margin remotely serrate, pubescent." — [PROSEA](https://plantuse.plantnet.org/en/Ocimum_tenuiflorum_(PROSEA))
- [P*] Inflorescence and flower: "Inflorescence racemose, 8-10 cm long, consisting of opposite 3-flowered cymes appearing as verticils, lax"; "flowers 3 mm long, pink or white." Fruit: 4 nutlets inside the persistent calyx on a spreading pedicel 3–4 mm long. — [PROSEA](https://plantuse.plantnet.org/en/Ocimum_tenuiflorum_(PROSEA))
- [P*] Thai vernacular names listed: "kaphrao (central), kom ko dong, im-khim-lam (northern)"; "Many forms have been described". — [PROSEA](https://plantuse.plantnet.org/en/Ocimum_tenuiflorum_(PROSEA))
- [P] Red vs white: holy basil is divided into two strains, กะเพราขาว and กะเพราแดง; white has stem and leaves "สีเขียวอมขาว" (green tinged white), red has stem and leaves "สีเขียวอมม่วงแดง" (green tinged purple-red), smells stronger than white and is preferred for medicine. (Introductory statement citing an earlier reference, in a Thammasat tissue-culture paper titled "…Ocimum sanctum L. (Holy Basil Purple Type)".) — [Autaijamsripon et al., Thai Science and Technology Journal 27(4), 2019](https://li01.tci-thaijo.org/index.php/tstj/article/download/189982/133065/559873)
- [P] Same paper: red holy basil cross-pollinates with other plants of the genus "เช่น กะเพราขาว โหระพา (O. basilicum) แมงลัก (O. americanum)", causing genetic variability. — [Autaijamsripon et al. 2019](https://li01.tci-thaijo.org/index.php/tstj/article/download/189982/133065/559873)
- [P] Mahidol University Faculty of Pharmacy herb-information article: กะเพรา is in LAMIACEAE, *Ocimum tenuiflorum* L., "เป็นไม้พุ่มขนาดเล็ก มี 2 สายพันธุ์คือ กะเพราขาวและกะเพราแดง"; leaf essential oil about 0.7% with eugenol about 71%. — [Mahidol MedPlant doc 156](https://medplant.mahidol.ac.th/document/156_Ocimum_tenuiflorum.pdf)
- [S] Tertiary cross-check (Wikipedia, citing a flora): erect many-branched subshrub 30–60 cm with hairy stems; leaves green or purple, petioled, ovate blade up to 5 cm, usually slightly toothed; purplish flowers in close whorls on elongated racemes. — [Wikipedia: Ocimum tenuiflorum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ocimum_tenuiflorum)
- [S] Leela Punyaratabandhu's photo caption: "Notice the jagged edges and fuzzy leaves and stems." — [SheSimmers, Pad Ka-Prao](https://shesimmers.com/2012/05/pad-ka-prao-%e0%b8%9c%e0%b8%b1%e0%b8%94%e0%b8%81%e0%b8%b0%e0%b9%80%e0%b8%9e%e0%b8%a3%e0%b8%b2.html)
- [U] eFloraofIndia forum comparison (unattributed to a named flora): leaves 2–4 cm, calyx glabrous within, fruiting calyx 4–5.5 mm, corolla 3–4 mm. — [eFloraofIndia](https://efloraofindia.com/efi/ocimum-tenuiflorum/)
- [U] Flora Malesiana text as returned in a search summary only (portal blocked): leaves elliptic-oblong 3–6 × 1–2.5 cm; "commonest form has a purple calyx and corolla, other forms have a green calyx and purple corolla, still others have a green calyx and white corolla". Note the summary also said margin "entire", which conflicts with PROSEA's "remotely serrate" — do not use until the page is read. — [Flora Malesiana portal](https://portal.cybertaxonomy.org/flora-malesiana/cdm_dataportal/taxon/1382dd07-b51b-4348-8e4b-5fc3c489eee0)
- [U] DTAM herb database entry "กะเพราแดง" reportedly gives leaf colour as green, purple-red tinged green, to purple-red. Snippet only; host unreachable. — [DTAM 0010](https://abdul.dtam.moph.go.th/thaiherbs/herb_pdf/0010.pdf)

### Inferences
- Drawing brief supported by sources: square-ish hairy stem woody at base (hairiness sourced; the square cross-section is sourced for *O. basilicum* and *O. americanum* in PROSEA, not explicitly for *O. tenuiflorum* in the text I received), opposite pairs at right angles, broadly elliptical blade with sparse shallow teeth, clear petiole up to about half the blade length, loose terminal spike of small whorled flowers.
- Flower colour "pink or white" (PROSEA) plus stem/leaf colour (Thai journal) is the defensible red/white visual contrast: red type = purple-red flush on stems and leaves; white type = pale green throughout.

### Gaps
- Stem cross-section and stem colour of *O. tenuiflorum* specifically, calyx colour, and calyx shape: not in any text I could read. Flora Malesiana/Flora of China would settle it; both blocked by bot checks.
- Kew POWO not opened (Cloudflare). Accepted-name status therefore still rests on GBIF from the first note.
- DOA's plant-variety (DUS) guideline for holy basil exists and would list official trait states (stem colour, hairiness, leaf shape, flower colour) but could not be read: https://www.doa.go.th/pvp/wp-content/uploads/2023/04/1holy-basil.pdf
- UBU phargarden and Kasetsart pages for กะเพรา were not located.

## 2. Wild holy basil (กะเพราป่า)

### Takeaway
No botanical or agricultural source defining กะเพราป่า was opened. The only non-cooking lead is a Department of Agriculture breeding report (snippet) that uses two "กะเพราป่า" lines as crossing parents with red and white holy basil, which implies DOA treats it as crossable holy-basil germplasm rather than a separate named species — but that is unverified. It should stay off screen as a botanical claim.

### Cited Findings
- [U] DOA research report "การสร้างลูกผสมกะเพราแดงสำหรับใช้ประกอบอาหาร": red holy basil was crossed with 2 white lines and 2 กะเพราป่า lines, giving 4 natural-cross combinations. Snippet only; PDF not readable (certificate error). — [DOA report 1302](https://doa.go.th/plan/wp-content/uploads/2021/04/1302การสร้างลูกผสมกะเพราแดงสำหรับใช้ประกอบอาหาร.pdf)
- [P] Krit Lueamlamai describes the pungent type he prefers without calling it "wild": sold at Isan vegetable stalls as small bundles of mature branches, "ใบเล็กหนา สีเขียว ก้านใบอ่อนสีออกม่วงเข้ม" (small thick green leaves, young petioles dark purplish), versus the common market type "ชนิดใบใหญ่ เนื้อใบอ่อน" (large, soft leaves). — [Matichon, 3 Feb 2018](https://www.matichon.co.th/lifestyle/news_827757)
- [S] CP RAM / Kasetsart Kamphaeng Saen PR article: Thailand has "more than 300" native holy-basil lines; four new cultivars (แก้วจุรี 01–04) were registered for protection in 2024. No definition of กะเพราป่า. — [Matichon PR, 2 Nov 2026](https://www.matichon.co.th/publicize/news_5958523)
- [S] In India, "vana (wild) tulsi" is commonly a different species, e.g. *Ocimum gratissimum* — this is about Indian usage and must not be transferred to the Thai market name. — [Wikipedia: Ocimum tenuiflorum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ocimum_tenuiflorum)

### Inferences
- Safe wording: "กะเพราป่า is a market/cook's name for small-leaved, strongly scented holy basil; no botanical source we could read defines it."

### Gaps
- Identity of กะเพราป่า (landrace of *O. tenuiflorum* vs another species) — OPEN. Best next step: read DOA reports 1301/1302/1304 at doa.go.th/plan (need a client that accepts the site's certificate).

## 3. Holy basil vs Thai sweet basil (โหระพา) vs lemon basil (แมงลัก) — visual differences

### Takeaway
PROSEA gives parallel descriptions that separate the three: holy basil has hairy, toothed, broadly elliptical leaves and a short lax spike of 3 mm pink/white flowers; sweet basil has nearly hairless, gland-dotted ovate leaves on stems from light green to dark purple with a long (to 30 cm) spike and 5–8 mm flowers; hoary/lemon basil (listed for Thailand as "maenglak") has entire-margined, hairless, lanceolate leaves on yellowish-green white-hairy stems with white flowers. Species naming for แมงลัก is inconsistent between sources.

### Cited Findings
- [P*] Sweet basil, *O. basilicum* (Thailand: "horapha"): stem quadrangular, glabrous or pilose when young, "light green to dark purple"; petiole 1–2(–4.5) cm; blade "ovate to elliptical", 1–5(–8) × 0.5–2(–4) cm, margin "entire to crenate-serrate", "densely glandular punctate, light green to purplish-green, glabrous to slightly pilose"; inflorescence terminal, lax, up to 30 cm, verticils 1–3 cm apart; corolla 5–8 mm, "whitish-purplish, white or creamy yellow"; nutlets 1–2 mm, black to dark brown. — [PROSEA, O. basilicum (Aguilar, Pham Truong Thi Tho & Oyen)](https://plantuse.plantnet.org/en/Ocimum_basilicum_(PROSEA))
- [P*] var. *thyrsiflorum* (the name often used for Thai basil) is described only as having an "inflorescence densely branched and with lax verticils". — [PROSEA, O. basilicum](https://plantuse.plantnet.org/en/Ocimum_basilicum_(PROSEA))
- [P*] *O. americanum* (Thailand: "maenglak"): stem and branches quadrangular, "yellowish-green, densely white-pilose in young parts"; petiole up to 2.5 cm; blade "lanceolate to elliptical, 2.5-5 cm × 1-2.5 cm", "margin entire", glabrous, gland-dotted both sides; inflorescence up to 15 cm, 6-flowered whorls up to 3 cm apart; corolla 4–6 mm, white; nutlets black; contains citral, camphor and methyl cinnamate in varying proportions. — [PROSEA, O. americanum (A.T. Sunarto)](https://plantuse.plantnet.org/en/Ocimum_americanum_(PROSEA))
- [P*] PROSEA notes var. *anisatum* is "now considered to be the hybrid" *O. × citriodorum* from a cross of *O. basilicum* and *O. americanum*. — [PROSEA, O. basilicum](https://plantuse.plantnet.org/en/Ocimum_basilicum_(PROSEA))
- [P] Thai journal usage: โหระพา = *O. basilicum*, แมงลัก = *O. americanum*. — [Autaijamsripon et al. 2019](https://li01.tci-thaijo.org/index.php/tstj/article/download/189982/133065/559873)
- [S] Pailin Chongchitnant: Thai basil "has dark green, pointy leaves, and often has purple stems", anise-like; holy basil leaves "are lighter green, more rounded, and more delicate", slightly peppery and milder in aroma. — [Hot Thai Kitchen](https://hot-thai-kitchen.com/pad-kra-pao-anything/)
- [S] Leela Punyaratabandhu: lemon basil is indispensable for khanom chin nam ya and kaeng liang, "no other kinds of basil can be used" for it, and it has a very short shelf life; holy and sweet basil can be swapped "in some cases (it is not ideal…)". — [SheSimmers, Lemon Basil](https://shesimmers.com/2011/08/lemon-basil-bai-maeng-lak-%e0%b9%83%e0%b8%9a%e0%b9%81%e0%b8%a1%e0%b8%87%e0%b8%a5%e0%b8%b1%e0%b8%81.html)

### Inferences
- Three-way illustrator key: (1) margin — holy: sparse teeth; sweet: entire to scalloped; lemon: entire. (2) hair — holy: hairy stem and leaf; sweet: nearly hairless, glossy; lemon: white hairs on young stems, leaf hairless. (3) stem colour — holy: green or purple-red flushed; sweet: light green to dark purple; lemon: yellowish-green. (4) flower — holy: tiny, pink/white, short spike; sweet: larger, whitish-purplish, long spike; lemon: white.

### Gaps
- Latin name for แมงลัก is unsettled: PROSEA and the Thai journal use *O. americanum*; the first note's GBIF check points to *O. × africanum* (syn. *O. × citriodorum*) for lemon basil. Print "แมงลัก (lemon basil)" without a Latin binomial, or state both.
- No Thai extension-service comparison sheet was read.

## 6. Canonical recipe and the disputes — what each authority actually says

### Takeaway
The blocked sources are now read. Key corrections to the first note: (a) the oldest written recipe found (M.L. Tiw, broadcast 7 Feb 1954) already contains sugar, salt, peppercorns, lard and holy-basil flowers — so "sugar is a later addition" is wrong; (b) Krit Lueamlamai reports that the early Chinese cook-shop version was seasoned with sweet black soy plus fish sauce, so dark soy is arguably older in practice than the fish-sauce-only "purist" version; (c) Pailin and Leela both state plainly that oyster and soy sauce are modern-standard and that purists use fish sauce only.

### Cited Findings
**Krit Lueamlamai (Matichon, 3 Feb 2018)**
- [P] The Facebook page "ทวงคืนผัดกะเพราไม่ใส่ถั่วฝักยาว ข้าวโพดอ่อน และหัวหอมใหญ่" created its phenomenon "ตั้งแต่ พ.ศ.2556" (2013). — [Matichon](https://www.matichon.co.th/lifestyle/news_827757)
- [P] He says the dish arose within only about "เจ็ดแปดสิบปี" (70–80 years); old cookbooks of a century ago never mention ผัดพริกใบกะเพรา; elders' accounts go back only to "กึ่งพุทธกาล" (c. B.E. 2500 / 1957), when meat stir-fried with chilli and holy basil was cooked by Chinese cooks at made-to-order shops, "ใช้ซีอิ๊วดำหวานปรุงรส เค็มน้ำปลา (บางทีก็เต้าเจี้ยวดำ) เผ็ดอ่อนๆ ด้วยพริกชี้ฟ้าหั่นแฉลบ แถมโรยพริกไทยป่นก่อนเสิร์ฟ" (seasoned with sweet black soy, salted with fish sauce or sometimes black bean sauce, mildly hot from sliced chi fa chillies, dusted with ground pepper). — [Matichon](https://www.matichon.co.th/lifestyle/news_827757)
- [P] His own method: pound phrik khi nu suan, Thai garlic and a little pepper coarsely; hot oil; fish sauce ("ชอบกลิ่นซีอิ๊วดำเค็มก็เพิ่มเอา" — add salty black soy if you like); high heat; holy basil last; no other sauces; served on rice with "น้ำปลาพริกขี้หนูมะนาว" and a crisp fried duck egg ("ไข่เป็ดดาวกรอบๆ"). Optional: a pinch of sun-dried holy basil leaves for deeper aroma. — [Matichon](https://www.matichon.co.th/lifestyle/news_827757)

**Silpa-Mag (19 Feb 2025), citing Narit Charatchanyawong, Silpa Wattanatham Oct 2024**
- [P] Oldest written recipe found: "เนื้อวัวผัดกะเพรา" by M.L. Tiw Chonlamakphichan (ม.ล. ติ๋ว ชลมารคพิจารณ์, 1901–1965), appointed head of the royal kitchen (หัวหน้าพระเครื่องต้น) of King Rama IX in 1954; from her radio talks on the palace station อ.ส.; "เนื้อไก่ผัดกะเพรา" aired 22 Sep 1953, "เนื้อวัวผัดกะเพรา" aired 7 Feb 1954; collected in the cremation book อาหารทางวิทยุ. — [Silpa-Mag](https://www.silpa-mag.com/culture/article_148492)
- [P] Her ingredients: finely chopped beef 2 cups; garlic about 10 cloves; phrik khi nu 1 heaped tsp; yellow chillies (พริกเหลือง) 10; picked holy basil leaves 1 cup; holy basil flowers 2 ช้อนคาว; sugar 1 ช้อนคาว; salt 1 tsp; peppercorns 20; fish sauce to taste; lard ¼ cup. Method: pound garlic with salt and pepper, bruise both chillies and the basil flowers, fry in hot lard, add meat, fish sauce and sugar, add basil leaves last; serves 4–6. — [Silpa-Mag](https://www.silpa-mag.com/culture/article_148492)
- [P] Narit's framing: this is the most original recipe "ในเชิงลายลักษณ์อักษร" (in written form); N. Na Paknam wrote in 1988 that กะเพราผัดพริก became popular only "30-odd years ago" (not before B.E. 2500); the long-bean recipe is by Bunnag Chowichian (นางบุนนาค ช่อวิเชียร), printed in the cremation volume for Khun Ueng Chantara on 30 March 1970 — almost 20 years after M.L. Tiw. — [Silpa-Mag](https://www.silpa-mag.com/culture/article_148492)

**Pailin Chongchitnant (Hot Thai Kitchen)**
- [S] "Modern" all-purpose recipe (2 servings): 10.5 oz protein; 1 tbsp oyster sauce; 1 tbsp soy sauce; 2 tsp fish sauce (+1 tsp on raw meat); ½ tsp Thai black or dark soy, optional ("to add colour and a richer flavour, but it is not necessary"); 2 tbsp water; 1½ tsp sugar; 1–3 Thai chillies; 5 garlic cloves; ¼ cup mild red chillies; ¼ medium onion ("optional… My other favorite veg to add is long beans"); 1½ cups holy basil. Method: pound chillies then garlic; sear meat; fry paste to golden; sauce; basil off the heat. — [Hot Thai Kitchen, Pad Kra Pao Anything](https://hot-thai-kitchen.com/pad-kra-pao-anything/)
- [S] "Old-school" recipe: the modern rendition "contains veggies like green beans and onions; along with… oyster sauce, soy sauce, and dark soy sauce"; in the traditional style "Fish sauce is your only seasoning", plus sugar, stock or water and ground pepper. - [S] Basil substitute: "Italian basil is my preferred substitute because it is a closer flavour match than Thai basil"; restaurants abroad may be using Thai basil. — [Hot Thai Kitchen](https://hot-thai-kitchen.com/pad-kra-pao-beef/)

**Leela Punyaratabandhu (SheSimmers, 8 May 2012)**

- [S] Her recipe (serves 2): 1 lb ground pork, beef or chicken; 7 large garlic cloves (26 g); 7 bird's-eye chillies (16 g); 1 large shallot (optional — "Many recipes don't call for them"); 2 tbsp oil; 1 tbsp fish sauce; 2 tbsp thin soy or seasoning sauce; 1 tbsp dark sweet soy; 1 tbsp oyster sauce; 1 cup packed holy basil. "I don't like sugar in my Pad Ka-Prao". No long beans, onion or baby corn. — [SheSimmers](https://shesimmers.com/2012/05/pad-ka-prao-%e0%b8%9c%e0%b8%b1%e0%b8%94%e0%b8%81%e0%b8%b0%e0%b9%80%e0%b8%9e%e0%b8%a3%e0%b8%b2.html)

### Cited Findings
**Prik nam pla (พริกน้ำปลา)**
- [S] Pailin: 3 tbsp fish sauce; 1–2 tbsp chopped Thai chillies; ½–1 tbsp lime juice; optional 2 sliced garlic cloves and 2 tbsp sliced shallots; rest 10 minutes if using garlic/shallot. Guideline "1 part lime juice to 3 parts fish sauce"; "some people don't even add any" lime. — [Hot Thai Kitchen, Prik Nam Pla](https://hot-thai-kitchen.com/prik-nam-pla/); [Hot Thai Kitchen, Pad Kra Pao Beef](https://hot-thai-kitchen.com/pad-kra-pao-beef/)
- [S] David Thompson: ¼ cup fish sauce; 10–15 sliced bird's-eye chillies; 2 sliced garlic cloves (optional); 1 tbsp lime juice (optional); pinch of chopped coriander. — [Gourmet Traveller](https://www.gourmettraveller.com.au/recipe/chefs-recipes/david-thompson-stir-fried-minced-beef-with-chillies-and-holy-basil-neua-pat-bai-grapao-7362/)
- [P] Krit: "น้ำปลาพริกขี้หนูมะนาว" (fish sauce, bird's-eye chilli, lime). — [Matichon](https://www.matichon.co.th/lifestyle/news_827757)

**Meat cut and fat**
- [S] Pailin: ground beef "regular or lean"; for chicken "use dark meat rather than breast so it will not be too dry"; for pork "stick with regular or lean, not extra lean"; in the cut-meat version pieces smaller than a normal stir-fry. No percentage given. — [Hot Thai Kitchen](https://hot-thai-kitchen.com/pad-kra-pao-beef/)
- [S] Leela: hand-chop 1 lb skinless chicken breast with a cleaver — "You get better texture that way." — [SheSimmers](https://shesimmers.com/2012/05/pad-ka-prao-%e0%b8%9c%e0%b8%b1%e0%b8%94%e0%b8%81%e0%b8%b0%e0%b9%80%e0%b8%9e%e0%b8%a3%e0%b8%b2.html)
- [S] Thompson: "coarsely minced beef"; M.L. Tiw: "เนื้อวัวสันละเอียด" with ¼ cup lard [P]. — [Gourmet Traveller](https://www.gourmettraveller.com.au/recipe/chefs-recipes/david-thompson-stir-fried-minced-beef-with-chillies-and-holy-basil-neua-pat-bai-grapao-7362/); [Silpa-Mag](https://www.silpa-mag.com/culture/article_148492)
- [S] Egg: Thompson fries in oil for "crispy, frazzled edges" with runny yolk; Pailin fries in about ¼ inch of oil; Krit specifies duck egg. — same sources as above.

**Thai standards for soy sauce and oyster sauce**
- [P] Ministry of Public Health notification "เรื่อง ผลิตภัณฑ์ปรุงรสที่ได้จากการย่อยโปรตีนของถั่วเหลือง", dated 6 May B.E. 2553 (2010), Royal Gazette vol. 127 special part 74 ง, 14 June 2010 (consolidated file name Compi_317); it repeals Notifications No. 202 (2000) and No. 248 (2001). Definition: a liquid product obtained by digesting soybean protein by fermentation or another suitable process, flavoured or coloured or not, "เช่น ซีอิ๊ว ซอสปรุงรส". — [Thai FDA consolidated notification](https://exfood.fda.moph.go.th/law/data/complilation/Compi_317.pdf)
- [P] Protein minimums: ≥ 4% by weight for fermented products with no added flavour or colour; ≥ 1.5% for fermented products with added flavour or colour; ≥ 10% for hydrolysed soy protein products. Iodine 2–3 mg/L where iodine is added. The notification does NOT define ซีอิ๊วขาว or ซีอิ๊วดำ separately. — [Thai FDA consolidated notification](https://exfood.fda.moph.go.th/law/data/complilation/Compi_317.pdf)
- [P] MOPH Notification No. 201 (2000) "ซอสบางชนิด" covers only chilli sauce, tomato sauce, papaya sauce, starch sauce and mixed sauce; oyster sauce is not mentioned in the extracted text. — [Thai FDA consolidated Notification 201](https://exfood.fda.moph.go.th/law/data/complilation/Compi_201.pdf)
- [S] Leela's working definitions: ซีอิ๊วขาว = light/thin soy ("literally 'white soy sauce'… nothing white about it"), thinner and gentler; ซีอิ๊วดำ = dark/"black" soy; ซีอิ๊วหวาน = sweet dark soy, close to Indonesian kecap; oyster sauce (น้ำมันหอย / ซ้อสหอยนางรม) is almost exclusively a cooking sauce; she uses Golden Mountain and Healthy Boy and calls them the two most prominent imported Thai soy brands in the US; she does not use Japanese-style soy in Thai cooking "unless in extreme cases". — [SheSimmers, Soy Sauces](https://shesimmers.com/2010/01/soy-sauces-used-in-thai-cooking-and-how.html)
- [S] Pailin: "I use Healthy Boy Thai soy sauce, but any regular soy sauce you have is fine." — [Hot Thai Kitchen](https://hot-thai-kitchen.com/pad-kra-pao-anything/)

**Fish sauce**
- [P] JIRCAS database (upgraded from [U]): fish normally used include *Stolephorus indicus*, *Stolephorus commersonii* (Sai-ton), *Clupeoides* sp. (Ka-tak) and others, including freshwater species; fish:salt ratio "from 5:1 to 1:1"; left 5 to 18 months; residue re-extracted 3–4 times with brine for lower grades blended with first-run liquid; produced on an industrial scale and exported to ASEAN, USA and Europe. No provinces named. — [JIRCAS nam-plaa](https://www.jircas.go.jp/en/database/thaifermented/nam-plaa)
- [U] Province leads, all snippet-level and non-official: several fish-sauce factories listed in Mae Klong, Samut Songkhram (map listing); Tiparos said to run factories in Chonburi (sector report); "54 fish sauce factories" nationwide (Wikipedia, old data). — [Longdo Map listing](https://map.longdo.com/poilist/72?page=789); [Wikipedia: Fishing industry in Thailand](https://www.Wikipedia.com/wiki/Fishing_industry_in_Thailand)

**Substitutes abroad**
- [S] Thai chillies → "another spicy chili such as serrano"; mild red chilli (phrik chi fa) → "red anaheim, long red pepper, mini bell pepper or even red bell pepper if all else fails" (bell pepper is "a bit too watery"). — [Hot Thai Kitchen, Beef](https://hot-thai-kitchen.com/pad-kra-pao-beef/); [Hot Thai Kitchen, Anything](https://hot-thai-kitchen.com/pad-kra-pao-anything/)
- [S] Thin soy → Golden Mountain seasoning sauce is interchangeable in Leela's recipe; Thai sweet dark soy → kecap manis. — [SheSimmers](https://shesimmers.com/2012/05/pad-ka-prao-%e0%b8%9c%e0%b8%b1%e0%b8%94%e0%b8%81%e0%b8%b0%e0%b9%80%e0%b8%9e%e0%b8%a3%e0%b8%b2.html)
- [S] Vegetarian: soy sauce in place of fish sauce; crumbled pressed/extra-firm tofu or plant-based mince. — [Hot Thai Kitchen](https://hot-thai-kitchen.com/pad-kra-pao-beef/)

### Inferences
- None of the five authorities read specifies Thai small-clove garlic or a substitute; all simply say "garlic" (Krit says "กระเทียมไทย"). Garlic origin is evidently not treated as critical by cooks writing for an overseas audience.
- No authority gives a fat percentage; "not extra lean" (Pailin) is the strongest statement available.
