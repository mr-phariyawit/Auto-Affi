## 1. Pork in Thailand: commercial crossbreeds vs native pigs; GI; cut, fat and hand-chopping for kra pao mince

### Takeaway
Ordinary Thai market pork comes from the same exotic breeds used worldwide (Duroc, Landrace, Large White and their crosses), so the pork itself is not what makes Thai kra pao taste different from a version cooked abroad; native pigs are rare, fatter and darker-red, and are a niche. I found no Thai authority that prescribes a specific cut or fat percentage for kra pao mince, and no fresh-pork GI — only a roast-pork product GI (หมูย่างเมืองตรัง).

### Cited Findings
**Commercial pork**
- [P] A 2025 Thai meat-science paper (KMITL + Betagro; Chaosap et al., *Animals* 15(17):2535) states "Exotic breeds like Duroc, Landrace and Large white, as well as their crosses are preferred for their desirable features" and that they are increasingly used on Thai commercial farms. It does not give a market-share figure. — [PMC12427217](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12427217/)
- [P] Same paper, intramuscular fat (ether extract, % of longissimus muscle): pure Duroc 4.02; Large White × Landrace (traditional line) 1.69; Large White × Landrace (line selected for marbling) 1.95; Duroc × LWLR crosses 2.37 and 3.00. Day-1 shear force 4.56 kg (Duroc) vs 6.01 kg (traditional LW×LR). Genotype mattered more than sex. — [PMC12427217](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12427217/)
- [P] The same authors say how these exotic breeds fit "Thai culinary and consumer preferences" is "not well documented" — i.e. the science has not tied breed to Thai dish flavour. — [PMC12427217](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12427217/)
- [S] The Thai Junior Encyclopedia (สารานุกรมไทยสำหรับเยาวชนฯ เล่ม 18 เรื่องที่ 9, republished on TruePlookpanya) lists the foreign breeds raised in Thailand as แลนด์เรซ (Landrace, Denmark), ลาร์จไวต์ (Large White / Yorkshire, England) and ดูร็อก (Duroc, USA), and says fattening pigs (หมูขุน) are crossbreds of these. — [TruePlookpanya 60540](https://www.trueplookpanya.com/knowledge/content/60540)

**Native pigs (หมูพื้นเมือง mu phuen mueang; popularly หมูดำ mu dam, "black pig")**
- [S] Four native types named in the Thai Junior Encyclopedia: หมูพันธุ์ไหหลำ (mu Hailam, "Hainan"; central and southern Thailand; black-and-white; best growth of the native types); หมูพันธุ์ควาย (mu Khwai, "buffalo pig"; North; mostly black; boars 125–150 kg, sows 100–125 kg, market weight ~80 kg); หมูพันธุ์ราด (mu Rad/Raad; Northeast; short compact body, slow-growing); หมูพันธุ์พวง (mu Phuang; Northeast; black, coarsest skin, lowest price, often crossed with Rad). — [TruePlookpanya 60540](https://www.trueplookpanya.com/knowledge/content/60540)
- [S] Same source's general description of native pigs: very few remain, mostly crossed with foreign breeds; small, slow-growing, "เนื้อแดงน้อยแต่มันมาก" (little lean, a lot of fat), short body, small ham and shoulder. — [TruePlookpanya 60540](https://www.trueplookpanya.com/knowledge/content/60540)
- [U] Silpa Wattanatham (ศิลปวัฒนธรรม) describes the northern Khwai pig as also called หมูตาขาว (mu ta khao, "white-eyed pig"), up to 160–180 kg, and the Rad pig (also called หมูกระโดน, mu Kradon) of the lower Northeast as 60–70 kg, small-boned, firm-fleshed. Page returned 403; snippet only. Note the weight for Khwai conflicts with the encyclopedia's 125–150 kg. — [silpa-mag.com](https://www.silpa-mag.com/?p=80299)
- [P] Peer-reviewed-level thesis data (Suranaree University of Technology, Jatuporn Khoonkaew, 2008, n = 12 native + 12 crossbred, slaughtered at 83.42 kg and 87.33 kg): Thai native pigs had thicker backfat (4.44 cm vs 2.53 cm, P<0.01), smaller loin-eye area (29.29 vs 55.54 cm², P<0.01), shorter carcass (66.75 vs 72.42 cm), higher muscle pH at 1 h and 24 h (6.94/5.86 vs 6.52/5.68), darker meat (L* 30.73/32.68 vs 33.36/38.29) and much redder meat (a* about 10.6/8.2 vs about 2.2–2.7). — [SUT thesis abstract PDF](https://sutir.sut.ac.th/jspui/bitstream/123456789/3129/1/abstract.pdf) (record: [sutir.sut.ac.th/…/3129](https://sutir.sut.ac.th/jspui/handle/123456789/3129))
  - Caveat read in the document: the Thai and English abstracts disagree on which muscle the pH was measured in (Thai: ham muscle; English: longissimus dorsi) and transpose the two crossbred a* values. The abstract does not name the native type, and reports no intramuscular fat, tenderness or taste-panel result.
- [U] A DLD research project aimed to develop a high-quality black pig for niche markets from Piétrain × native (PN) crosses, targeting tenderness and intramuscular fat. Snippet only; project document not opened. — [TSRI e-library RDG5920002 abstract](https://elibrary.tsri.or.th/fullP/RDG5920002/RDG5920002_abstract.pdf)

**GI (สิ่งบ่งชี้ทางภูมิศาสตร์)**
- [P] หมูย่างเมืองตรัง (mu yang mueang Trang, Trang roast pork) is a registered Thai GI: Thai PBS, 1 Nov 2023, quoting the Director-General of the Department of Intellectual Property, lists it with พริกไทยตรัง as Trang's two earlier GIs before ข้าวเบายอดม่วงตรัง (reg. no. สช 66100212). This is a cooked product, not a breed or fresh pork. The article gives no registration number or date for the pork. — [Thai PBS](https://www.thaipbs.or.th/news/content/333417)
- [U] Other livestock GIs that surfaced are beef, not pork: เนื้อโคขุนโพนยางคำ (Sakon Nakhon), Kamphaeng Saen beef, Surin beef. — [data.go.th](https://data.go.th/en/dataset/snk_moc_661_03), [DIP GI-96-268](https://www.ipthailand.go.th/images/26881/GI-96-268.pdf), [DIP GI-95-267](https://www.ipthailand.go.th/images/26881/GI-95-267.pdf)

**Cut, fat and hand-chopping for kra pao**
- [S] Pailin Chongchitnant (Hot Thai Kitchen): for pork "stick with regular or lean, not extra lean"; no percentage. — [Hot Thai Kitchen](https://hot-thai-kitchen.com/pad-kra-pao-beef/) (carried from gapfill notes)
- [S] Leela Punyaratabandhu (SheSimmers) hand-chops the meat with a cleaver: "You get better texture that way." Her statement is about chicken breast. — [SheSimmers](https://shesimmers.com/2012/05/pad-ka-prao-%e0%b8%9c%e0%b8%b1%e0%b8%94%e0%b8%81%e0%b8%b0%e0%b9%80%e0%b8%9e%e0%b8%a3%e0%b8%b2.html) (carried from gapfill notes)
- [U] A Thermomix/Cookidoo "ผัดกะเพราหมูสับ" recipe specifies pork neck/collar (เนื้อหมูส่วนสันคอ, san kho), diced and part-frozen before chopping, 250 g. A commercial appliance recipe, not a culinary authority. — [Cookidoo](https://cookidoo.international/recipes/recipe/en/r929228)
- [U] Grantourismo says most Thai stalls make their own mince; several English-language blogs suggest roughly 10–25% fat. None is a Thai authority. — [Grantourismo](https://grantourismotravels.com/thai-pad-kra-pao-recipe/)

### Inferences
- Because Thai commodity pork is Duroc/Landrace/Large White crossbred like pork in the US, EU, UK and Australia, the channel can truthfully tell viewers that supermarket pork abroad is essentially the same animal; the differences worth teaching are fat level, coarseness of the mince and freshness, not breed.
- Native "black pig" would give a fattier, darker, redder mince (backfat nearly double in the SUT study), but it is a speciality product; presenting it as "what Thais normally use" would be false.
- Lean-selected white-line pork measured only ~1.7–2% intramuscular fat in the Thai study, which is consistent with cooks' advice not to buy extra-lean mince.

### Gaps
- **หมูกี้ (mu Ki)** and the spelling "Kwai": I found no source at all for a native type called หมูกี้; the buffalo-pig type is written หมูพันธุ์ควาย. Do not use หมูกี้ on screen until a source is found.
- No DLD (กรมปศุสัตว์ / สำนักพัฒนาพันธุ์สัตว์) breed page was reached; the four-type list rests on the Thai Junior Encyclopedia.
- No market-share figure for crossbred vs native pork in Thailand.
- No Thai study found comparing native vs commercial pork on intramuscular fat, flavour compounds or taste panel.
- No Thai culinary authority found stating a cut (สันคอ san kho collar / สะโพก saphok ham / สามชั้น sam chan belly) or a fat percentage for kra pao mince, and none found explaining hand-chopped pork (หมูสับมือ) vs machine-ground with reasons. Two Thai-language searches returned only user recipes. This remains craft knowledge without a citable source.
- GI register itself was not searched directly (ipthailand.go.th PDFs did not render); "no fresh-pork GI" is an absence of evidence, not a verified negative.

## 2. Chicken: native (ไก่บ้าน / ไก่พื้นเมือง) vs commercial broiler; which part for kra pao

### Takeaway
Thai meat science consistently shows native chicken is leaner, higher in protein and collagen, and measurably tougher than broiler; one paper found slightly more glutamic acid. Evidence on flavour itself is thin. For kra pao the recommended substitute abroad is simply dark meat (thigh), per Pailin.

### Cited Findings
- [U] Wattanachant, Benjakul & Ledward (2004), *Poultry Science* 83(1):123–128 (Prince of Songkla University / University of Reading): Thai indigenous chicken muscle had higher protein and lower fat and ash than broiler (P<0.001); slightly more glutamic acid (P<0.05); more saturated and less polyunsaturated fatty acids; total collagen 5.09 mg/g (breast) and 12.85 mg/g (thigh) vs 3.86 and 8.70 mg/g in broiler; soluble collagen lower (22.16% vs 31.38% breast; 26.06% vs 33.87% thigh); shear values higher raw and cooked; cooking lowered shear in broiler but not in indigenous thigh. Abstract seen via search snippet only — repository page blocked. Breed line not named in the abstract. — [University of Reading repository](https://centaur.reading.ac.uk/12868/)
- [P] Pongduang, Leotaragul, Pianmongkol, Thakeow & Jaturasitha (2013), *Khon Kaen Agriculture Journal* pp. 21–26 (Chiang Mai University group): 240 birds at 1.2–1.3 kg; ไก่ประดู่หางดำเชียงใหม่ 1 (kai Pradu Hang Dam Chiang Mai 1) and its crossbred had higher shear force than broiler in breast and thigh; Pradu Hang Dam thigh had the highest soluble, insoluble and total collagen; authors conclude "Pradu Hang Dam meat was the toughest" and suits consumers who like a chewy texture. The abstract gives no numbers. — [FAO AGRIS record](https://agris.fao.org/search/en/records/64738d7968b4c299a3f88bfd)
- [U] Kaewkot, Ruangsuriya, Kreuzer & Jaturasitha (*Animal Production Science*, AN18759): Ross broiler meat had lower shear force, higher intramuscular fat and higher drip loss than Pradu Hang Dam, Rhode Island Red and their crossbred; broiler oxidative stability was weaker. Publisher page returned 404; snippet only. — [CSIRO Publishing](https://publish.csiro.au/an/AN18759)
- [U] DLD and the Thailand Research Fund selected four purebred native lines — ประดู่หางดำ (Pradu Hang Dam, "black-tailed Pradu"), เหลืองหางขาว (Lueang Hang Khao, "yellow, white-tailed"), ชี (Chi/Chee) and แดง (Daeng/Dang, "red"); one MDPI paper dates the project to 2002, a PMC paper dates the Kabinburi (กบินทร์บุรี) centre programme to 2003. Snippets only. — [MDPI Sustainability 16(17):7811](https://www.mdpi.com/2071-1050/16/17/7811/htm); [PMC6601067](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6601067)
- [U] ไก่แดงสุราษฎร์ (kai Daeng Surat) is registered with DLD as a poultry breed, no. CND 03/2010, dated 9 Dec 2010 (B.E. 2553). Snippet only. — [Khon Kaen Agr. J. (ThaiJO)](https://li01.tci-thaijo.org/index.php/agkasetkaj/article/view/250107)
- [U] A DIP GI document exists for ไก่เบตง (kai Betong, Yala): native Betong breed, yellowish-white skin, "firm, tender" meat, males 1.50–2.20 kg dressed, chicks from DLD centres. I could not extract the PDF text, so whether it is a granted registration or an application notice is unconfirmed. — [DIP GI-98-272](https://www.ipthailand.go.th/images/26881/GI-98-272.pdf)
- [S] Which part for kra pao: Pailin — for chicken "use dark meat rather than breast so it will not be too dry". Leela uses hand-chopped skinless breast. The two authorities differ. — [Hot Thai Kitchen](https://hot-thai-kitchen.com/pad-kra-pao-beef/); [SheSimmers](https://shesimmers.com/2012/05/pad-ka-prao-%e0%b8%9c%e0%b8%b1%e0%b8%94%e0%b8%81%e0%b8%b0%e0%b9%80%e0%b8%9e%e0%b8%a3%e0%b8%b2.html)

### Inferences
- The documented native-vs-broiler difference is mainly **texture** (more collagen, less soluble collagen, higher shear force, less fat). A script may say native chicken is firmer and chewier; it should not claim a proven flavour-compound difference beyond "slightly higher glutamic acid in one study".
- A quick high-heat mince stir-fry does not dissolve collagen, so native chicken's chew would survive in kra pao; viewers abroad using broiler thigh get a softer result. That is an inference, not a tested finding.

### Gaps
- No source found saying Thai street kra pao kai is normally made with native chicken; everyday use of broiler is likely but uncited.
- No peer-reviewed sensory/flavour-volatile comparison was opened. Thai work on inosine monophosphate, anserine/carnosine in native chicken exists in the literature but was not retrieved in this session — do not cite it from this note.
- Full names "เหลืองหางขาวกบินทร์", "ชีท่าพระ" and their DLD registration details were not verified on a primary page.
- Wattanachant 2004 numbers need a primary read (PubMed abstract) before going on screen.

## 3. Eggs: chicken vs duck for ไข่ดาว (khai dao), and the Thai deep-fry method

### Takeaway
The Thai fried egg is shallow/deep-fried in a generous pool of very hot oil so it puffs and the white's edge turns golden and crisp; duck or chicken egg are both used, duck being described as richer. I found no food-science source explaining the lacy edge specifically, only general protein-denaturation explanations.

### Cited Findings
- [P] Praew (Thai-born cook, Hungry in Thailand): 1 cup neutral oil; heat until "very hot and shimmering, almost smoking"; egg should puff and sizzle immediately; leave alone for a runny yolk, or spoon hot oil over the top for a firmer yolk; duck or chicken egg both fine, duck yolks creamier and richer; very popular on pad kra pao. No mechanism given. — [Hungry in Thailand](https://hungryinthailand.com/thai-fried-egg-kai-dao/)
- [S] Pailin fries in about ¼ inch of oil; Thompson wants "crispy, frazzled edges" with a runny yolk; Krit Luealamai serves kra pao with a crisp fried duck egg (ไข่เป็ด khai pet). — sources in `ingredient_cards_gapfill.md` line 166/222: [Hot Thai Kitchen](https://hot-thai-kitchen.com/pad-kra-pao-beef/)
- [U] Tasting Table: Thai-style eggs are fried in plenty of very hot oil until the edges are crisp and golden; the wok should be near smoking and oil ladled over the egg. — [Tasting Table](https://www.tastingtable.com/2211764/crispy-fried-eggs-thai-style/)
- [U] America's Test Kitchen publishes a "Kai Dao (crispy fried eggs)" recipe using large chicken eggs. — [ATK](https://www.americastestkitchen.com/recipes/14921-kai-dao-crispy-fried-eggs)
- [U] General science (Thai education site): heat breaks the hydrogen bonds holding egg-white proteins folded, so the white turns opaque and firm. Not specific to crisp edges. — [TruePlookpanya 61211](https://www.trueplookpanya.com/knowledge/content/61211/-blo-sciche-sci-)

### Inferences
- The crisp lacy edge is plausibly the thin outer white dehydrating and browning in oil far above 100 °C while steam puffs it; this is standard frying physics but **no opened source states it for khai dao**, so present it as explanation, not citation.
- "Duck egg is the traditional choice" is supported only by individual cooks (Krit; Praew says either). Safer wording: "many Thai cooks prefer duck egg; chicken egg is equally common."

### Gaps
- No sourced comparison of duck vs chicken egg composition in the Thai-frying context (yolk ratio, fat) was retrieved.
- No oil temperature figure from a credible Thai source.
- Claim (seen in a snippet) that the name ไข่ดาว "star egg" comes from the puffed star-like edge is unverified etymology — do not use.

## 4. Other proteins used for kra pao in Thailand

### Takeaway
Beef is the best-documented alternative (it is the oldest written recipe); general Thai sources say pork, chicken, beef or seafood are all used. I found no respected source documenting crispy pork belly or century-egg kra pao specifically.

### Cited Findings
- [S] Oldest written recipe found is beef: "เนื้อวัวผัดกะเพรา" (nuea wua phat kaphrao) by M.L. Tiw Chonlamakphichan, head of King Rama IX's royal kitchen. — [Silpa Wattanatham](https://www.silpa-mag.com/?p=148492) (carried from gapfill notes; reconfirmed in search snippet)
- [S] Thompson's recipe uses 200 g coarsely minced beef; Pailin gives pork, beef or chicken as interchangeable. — `ingredient_cards_gapfill.md` lines 180–184; [Hot Thai Kitchen](https://hot-thai-kitchen.com/pad-kra-pao-beef/)
- [U] Tatler Asia (Thai edition), best kaprao in Bangkok: the dish can use any meat — beef, pork, chicken and seafood; one restaurant serves a 30-day dry-aged beef version. — [Tatler Asia](https://www.tatlerasia.com/dining/food/best-pad-kaprao-in-bangkok-th)
- [U] A blog source notes versions with chunks of chicken or crispy roast pork "are not uncommon". Not an authority. — [Grantourismo](https://grantourismotravels.com/thai-pad-kra-pao-recipe/)

### Inferences
- The channel can safely say pork, chicken and beef are the core proteins and seafood is common; หมูกรอบ (mu krop, crispy pork belly) and ไข่เยี่ยวม้า (khai yiao ma, century egg) versions are widely sold in Thailand but should be presented as popular modern variants without historical claims.

### Gaps
- No respected documentary source found for กะเพราหมูกรอบ, กะเพราทะเล or กะเพราไข่เยี่ยวม้า (origin, date, standard method).

## 5. For viewers abroad: how mince is labelled and what to buy

### Takeaway
UK and Australian supermarket pork mince is sold by stated fat percentage (UK commonly 5%, also 10/12/20%; Australia regular about 15%, extra-lean about 5%); US ground pork often carries a lean/fat ratio such as 80/20. The Thai-cooking advice is to avoid the extra-lean packs and, for chicken, buy thigh.

### Cited Findings
- [U] UK: Tesco Lean Pork Mince "5% Fat"; Tesco Woodside Farms "12% Fat" pork mince; Sainsbury's British pork mince at 5% and 20% fat (a price tracker also lists 10%). — [Tesco 5%](https://www.tesco.com/groceries/en-GB/products/299259490); [Tesco 12%](https://www.tesco.com/shop/en-GB/products/292286358); [Sainsbury's 5%](https://www.sainsburys.co.uk/gol-ui/product/sainsburys-british-pork-mince-5-fat-500g); [Sainsbury's 20%](https://www.sainsburys.co.uk/gol-ui/product/sainsburys-british-pork-mince-20-fat-500g)
- [U] EU/UK rule (Regulation (EU) 1169/2011 Annex VI Part B, as summarised in UK butchers' guidance): "lean minced meat" ≤7% fat; "minced meat containing pork" ≤30% fat and collagen/meat-protein ratio ≤18. Guidance PDF returned 404 when opened; snippet only; verify against the regulation text. — [Darlington BC butchers' guidance (2014)](https://www.darlington.gov.uk/media/1583/butchers_guidance_-_fic_2014.pdf)
- [U] Australia: Woolworths Pork Mince label "85% meat, 15% fat" typical; Woolworths Heart Smart Extra Lean Pork Mince ≤5% fat; Coles "3 Star" regular pork mince <15% fat (third-party page). — [Woolworths pork mince](https://www.woolworths.com.au/shop/productdetails/169853/woolworths-pork-mince); [Woolworths extra lean](https://www.woolworths.com.au/shop/productdetails/666557/woolworths-heart-smart-extra-lean-pork-mince)
- [U] USA: retail example "Ground Pork 80% Lean 20% Fat" (Safeway Open Nature); USDA FSIS requires a fat percentage wherever a lean percentage is claimed on ground product, and its sample panel shows "Pork, ground, 91% lean, 9% fat". — [Safeway](https://shop.safeway.com/product-details.960290117.html); [FSIS nutrition labelling Q&A](https://www.fsis.usda.gov/sites/default/files/media_file/2021-07/Nutrition_labeling_Q_A_041312_0.pdf)
- [S] Thai-cooking authority advice: Pailin — pork/beef "regular or lean, not extra lean"; chicken dark meat; Leela — chop it yourself for texture. — [Hot Thai Kitchen](https://hot-thai-kitchen.com/pad-kra-pao-beef/); [SheSimmers](https://shesimmers.com/2012/05/pad-ka-prao-%e0%b8%9c%e0%b8%b1%e0%b8%94%e0%b8%81%e0%b8%b0%e0%b9%80%e0%b8%9e%e0%b8%a3%e0%b8%b2.html)

### Inferences
- Mapping Pailin's "regular, not extra lean" onto labels: avoid UK 5% / AU extra-lean 5% / US 90+% lean; choose UK 10–20%, AU regular (~15%), US 80/20. This mapping is mine, not stated by any source.
- "Ask the butcher for coarsely ground or hand-chop pork shoulder/collar" is reasonable (shoulder is the usual fatty mincing cut) but no Thai authority I opened says it in those words.

### Gaps
- No legal maximum fat for US "ground pork" was confirmed (an old article's "30%" figure is beef-related/dated — do not use).
- All retailer label figures are snippet-level; product ranges change, so re-check before putting percentages on screen.
- Nothing found for continental EU retail labelling beyond the regulation ceiling.

## 6. What is still unknown or disputed

### Takeaway
The strongest truthful story is: same pig breeds as abroad, so teach fat level and coarse chop; native chicken is firmer by measurement; duck egg and deep oil are cook's practice. Most "why it tastes different" claims about breed are not supported by evidence I could open.

### Cited Findings
- [P] Thai researchers themselves say the link between exotic pig breeds and Thai culinary preference is "not well documented". — [PMC12427217](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12427217/)
- [P]/[S] Disputed between authorities: chicken breast, hand-chopped (Leela) vs dark meat (Pailin). — [SheSimmers](https://shesimmers.com/2012/05/pad-ka-prao-%e0%b8%9c%e0%b8%b1%e0%b8%94%e0%b8%81%e0%b8%b0%e0%b9%80%e0%b8%9e%e0%b8%a3%e0%b8%b2.html); [Hot Thai Kitchen](https://hot-thai-kitchen.com/pad-kra-pao-beef/)
- [S]/[U] Conflicting native-pig data: Khwai pig adult weight 125–150 kg (encyclopedia) vs 160–180 kg (Silpa Wattanatham snippet). — [TruePlookpanya 60540](https://www.trueplookpanya.com/knowledge/content/60540); [silpa-mag.com](https://www.silpa-mag.com/?p=80299)

### Inferences
- Any on-screen claim that "Thai pork tastes different because of the breed" would currently be unsupported; freshness (warm, same-day wet-market meat vs chilled packaged mince) is a commonly asserted reason but I found no source for it in this session.

### Gaps
- Open items needing a primary read before scripting: DLD breed pages (pig and chicken); DIP GI register entries for ไก่เบตง and ไข่เค็มไชยา (both snippet-only); Wattanachant 2004 abstract; any Thai chef statement on pork cut, fat ratio and หมูสับมือ; existence of หมูกี้.
- Suggested follow-up with a real browser: breeding.dld.go.th, ipthailand.go.th GI search, PubMed PMID for Wattanachant 2004, krua.co kra pao articles.
