## 3. Retention mechanics — what the evidence does and does not support

### Takeaway
The structural reading (question hook, open loop, visible counter, escalation, payoff) fits the observed data pattern but is an interpretation; I found no primary study proving each element. Published first-party guidance I could reach is thin: TikTok's ads creative guide stresses the opening seconds, and YouTube's own policy text says templated repetition is a monetisation risk. Popular statistics about "3-second hooks" and "optimal length" circulate without traceable primary sources and should not be relied on.

### Cited Findings
- TikTok's advertiser creative guide ("Creative Codes") states that the first six seconds are critical for the hook. [SEC — surfaced in a search summary; I did not open the PDF] — [TikTok for Business, Creative Codes](https://ads.tiktok.com/business/library/Creative_Codes_ENG.pdf)
- Widely repeated figures (63% of top click-through videos hook within three seconds; 71% of users decide within three seconds; 7-15 s videos outperform) appeared only in marketing blogs with no primary link. [SEC, unverified] — [Marketing Brew report on a TikTok attention study](https://www.marketingbrew.com/stories/2023/12/18/pay-attention-to-me-report-shares-brand-tips-for-catching-users-interest-on-tiktok); [Soundstripe](https://www.soundstripe.com/blogs/how-to-get-more-views-on-tiktok)
- Observed format constants on @mathmango1: 23/24 question captions, 18-30 s length, original sound on every video. [OBS] — [TikTok profile](https://www.tiktok.com/@mathmango1)
- Observed engagement mix on the clacks video: saves 7.9K > shares 2.5K > comments 872 on 4.4M plays. [OBS] — [clacks video](https://www.tiktok.com/@mathmango1/video/7696572112902950166)
- On the 19.6M Moon-folds video: 52.2K saves, 22.1K shares, 7.2K comments. [OBS] — [Moon folds video](https://www.tiktok.com/@mathmango1/video/7699020033304890646)

### Inferences (labelled as interpretation, not proven)
- Question caption + persistent on-screen question = the viewer knows from frame one what the video will answer (open loop).
- A counter gives a second-by-second reason to keep watching, and three escalating rounds (3 -> 31 -> 314) let the viewer form and test a prediction, with the pattern only visible at the end; this rewards full watch-through and a rewatch.
- 25-30 s is long enough for three rounds and short enough to be finished; the channel's only notably shorter video (18 s, Earths in the Sun) did fine (724K), so there is no evidence in this data that 27 s is "optimal" versus 18 s.
- No voice and almost no language means no translation barrier; English on-screen text is short. This plausibly explains global reach but also means the audience is not country-specific.
- Saves outnumber comments 9:1 on "clacks", so "people arguing in comments" is not the main visible signal here.

### Gaps
- No official TikTok 2025-2026 document on watch-through rate, optimal length or rewatch weighting was reached. TikTok's Creator Academy page returned only navigation chrome.
- No credible independent analytics study (with method and sample) on hook timing or optimal length in 2025-2026 was found in the search budget.
- Loopability of the clacks video (whether the end cuts cleanly into the start) was not observed.

## 5. Platform risk — unoriginal, repetitive, mass-produced, AI

### Takeaway
The main risk for a templated simulation channel is YouTube's "inauthentic content" monetisation rule, whose wording (looks made from a template; low educational value; repetitive across videos) describes this genre's failure mode closely. On TikTok, code-rendered animation is not what the AI-label rule targets (realistic AI media), but reused/unoriginal content is reportedly excluded from the For You feed.

### Cited Findings
- YouTube renamed its "repetitious content" policy to "inauthentic content" on 2025-07-15 and says it covers repetitive or mass-produced content. [OBS-FETCH] — [YouTube channel monetisation policies](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392?hl=en)
- YouTube's definition: content that looks made with a template or feels repetitive after several videos from the same channel. Not allowed: similar/repetitive content with low educational value; AI-generated content built on generic or unoriginal templates. Allowed: series where each video has a distinct storyline, focus or concept; shared intros/outros. [OBS-FETCH] — [YouTube channel monetisation policies](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392?hl=en)
- YouTube's reused-content rule applies channel-wide to content already online without significant original commentary; permission from the original creator does not exempt it. [OBS-FETCH] — [YouTube channel monetisation policies](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392?hl=en)
- YouTube allows automated/AI tools where the final product still shows the creator's own creative vision. [OBS-FETCH] — [YouTube channel monetisation policies](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392?hl=en)
- YouTube's creator liaison described the July 2025 change as a minor clarification; such content was already ineligible. [SEC] — [Search Engine Journal](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/youtube-monetization-update-spam-not-reaction-channels/550755/)
- TikTok defines AI-generated content as images, video or audio generated or modified by AI. It requires a label on AI content containing realistic images, audio or video, and encourages a label on content fully generated or significantly edited by AI. Unlabelled realistic AI content may be removed. [OBS-FETCH] — [TikTok Support: About AI-generated content](https://www.tiktok.com/support/faq_detail?id=7636670084747893268)
- TikTok may auto-label uploads carrying C2PA Content Credentials, and an auto label cannot be removed. Wrongly applying the creator AI label to unaltered content breaches TikTok's terms. [OBS-FETCH] — [TikTok Support: About AI-generated content](https://www.tiktok.com/support/faq_detail?id=7636670084747893268)
- None of the 24 MathMango videos carries an AI label in page data. [OBS] — [TikTok profile](https://www.tiktok.com/@mathmango1)
- Since November 2025 TikTok users can reduce AI-generated content in their For You feed via a topic slider. [SEC] — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/11/18/tiktok-now-lets-you-choose-how-much-ai-generated-content-you-want-to-see/)
- TikTok's For You feed eligibility standards reportedly make unoriginal/reused content (e.g. another account's watermark, no creative edits) ineligible for recommendation, with account-level ineligibility for repeat cases; one source dates this to 2025-09-13. [SEC, date unconfirmed] — [Relevant Audience](https://www.relevantaudience.com/tiktok-ads/tiktok-algorithm-for-you-feed/); [ConductAtlas provision record](https://conductatlas.com/platform/tiktok/tiktok-community-guidelines/for-you-feed-eligibility-exclusion/)

### Inferences
- A simulation rendered by your own code (physics computed, frames drawn programmatically) is not "generated or modified by AI" under TikTok's definition, even if an AI assistant helped write the code; the label rule is about the media itself. This is my reading of the policy text, not a TikTok ruling. If AI image/video/voice tools produce the visuals or audio, labelling applies.
- On YouTube, 24 near-identical 27-second videos with one caption pattern is the shape the "template" wording describes. Mitigation consistent with the policy text: each video a distinct concept with real explanatory value (the reveal explains why), not re-skins of one simulation.
- Re-uploading someone else's simulation footage is a reused/unoriginal-content problem on both platforms; re-implementing the same idea in your own code and design is not reuse of their content.

### Gaps
- TikTok's Community Guidelines text on For You eligibility and originality was not read directly.
- No official statement from either platform specifically about code-generated or procedurally animated videos was found.

## 6. Originality and IP — Galperin, 3Blue1Brown, and what is protectable

### Takeaway
The pi-from-collisions result is a mathematical fact published by Gregory Galperin in 2003 and popularised by 3Blue1Brown; facts, ideas and methods are outside copyright, while a specific video's animation, music, wording and design are protected expression. The safe practice is to build your own simulation and visuals, use your own or licensed audio, and credit Galperin and 3Blue1Brown in the caption.

### Cited Findings
- G. Galperin, "Playing Pool with pi (The Number pi from a Billiard Point of View)", Regular and Chaotic Dynamics 8:4 (2003), pp. 375-394: counting collisions in a two-ball billiard system yields the digits of pi to any accuracy. [SEC, bibliographic record] — [Math-Net.ru record](https://mathnet.ru/eng/rcd790); [paper PDF](https://www.maths.tcd.ie/~lebed/Galperin.%20Playing%20pool%20with%20pi.pdf)
- 3Blue1Brown's explainer, by Grant Sanderson, popularised the block-collision "clacks" version; sources disagree on whether it was released in 2018 or early 2019. [SEC] — [3Blue1Brown lesson](https://3blue1brown.com/lessons/clacks-solution); [Futility Closet, Feb 2019](https://www.futilitycloset.com/2019/02/05/pi-clacks/); [That's Maths, May 2019](https://thatsmaths.com/2019/05/09/bouncing-billiard-balls-produce-pi/)
- US copyright law (17 U.S.C. 102(b)) excludes ideas, procedures, processes, systems, methods of operation, concepts, principles and discoveries from protection, however they are described or illustrated. [SEC] — [Stanford CIS on section 102(b)](https://cyberlaw.stanford.edu/blog/2007/08/section-102b-and-negative-categories-copyright-subject-matter/)
- Under the merger and scènes-à-faire doctrines, expression that is the only practical way to show an idea, or stock elements that follow from a theme, is not protected; the line is drawn case by case. [SEC] — [Wikipedia, idea-expression distinction](https://en.wikipedia.com/wiki/Idea-expression_divide); [Mondaq commentary](https://www.mondaq.com/unitedstates/copyright/1398742/two-courts-two-ad-campaigns-and-copyrights-idea-expression-dichotomy)
- MathMango's caption for the clacks video contains no credit to Galperin or 3Blue1Brown (caption is the title plus five hashtags). [OBS] — [clacks video](https://www.tiktok.com/@mathmango1/video/7696572112902950166)

### Inferences
- Not protectable: the fact that mass ratios of 100^n give collision counts matching pi's digits; the physics; the idea of two blocks and a wall; a counter; a question-then-reveal structure; the 1 / 100 / 10,000 kg progression (dictated by the maths).
- Protectable and not to be copied: another creator's actual footage, their specific colour scheme/typography/layout taken together, their audio, their exact on-screen wording, the word "clacks" as used alongside 3Blue1Brown-like visuals (the term itself is ordinary, but lifting title plus look together is closer to copying expression).
- Good practice: write your own simulation; pick a visibly different visual design; own or licensed sound; credit line such as "Result: G. Galperin (2003); popularised by 3Blue1Brown" in the caption; in Thai if the audience is Thai. Crediting is ethics and audience trust, not a legal licence, and it does not fix a copied video.
- This is general information, not legal advice; Thai copyright law was not researched here.

### Gaps
- Thailand's Copyright Act treatment of ideas versus expression was not researched.
- Whether MathMango's visuals resemble 3Blue1Brown's was not assessed (videos not viewed).
- Whether the "original sound" contains third-party music is unknown.
