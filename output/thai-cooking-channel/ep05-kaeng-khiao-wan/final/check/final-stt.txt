This is kaeng khiao wan, Thai green curry. It first shows up in a Thai cookbook from 1926. The very first Thai cookbooks, from the early 1890s, mention other curries, but not this one. The green comes from fresh green chilis, which is unusual because most other Thai curries use red chilis. Wan means sweet, but here it describes a soft pale shade of green, not the taste. Use Thai green chilis or Serrano peppers for heat, plus milder green chilis, like Korean green peppers or jalapeños, for more chili flavor without too much heat. Wear gloves and remove the seeds if you like it milder. Toast coriander seeds and cumin seeds, then grind them into a fine powder with white peppercorns. A coffee grinder gets them really fine. For the herbs, use only the bottom half of the lemongrass, never dried or powdered. Fresh or frozen galangal is best. Add makrut lime zest or the zest of half a lime, and coriander root. In a large stone mortar, pound the chilis with coarse salt first, then the herbs, then shallots, garlic, and the dry spices. An immersion blender works too. Finish with fermented shrimp paste called kapi. Pailin says it is always added to traditional Thai curry pastes. A few Thai basil leaves boost the green color, while their aroma cooks away. Short on time? Store-bought paste is fine, and most Thai people buy it pre-made too. Most commercial pastes skip shrimp paste, so add a little. Brands vary hugely in strength, so taste as you go. Bring some coconut milk to a full boil. Add the paste and fry it, stirring often, until the coconut oil starts to separate. Those little green dots of oil show the paste has released its flavor. Add chicken thigh, the most common choice and the tastiest, then the rest of the coconut milk, stock, fish sauce, and palm sugar. Bruise the makrut lime leaves and tear them in. Thai eggplant goes in next. Cook it until a fork goes through with slight resistance. No Thai eggplant? Canned bamboo shoots work. Just give them a rinse. Stir in Thai basil just until it wilts. Taste and add more fish sauce if needed. It should be spicy and salty with only a lick of sweetness from the coconut. Serve with jasmine rice. If in Thailand, affiliate links are in the caption.
