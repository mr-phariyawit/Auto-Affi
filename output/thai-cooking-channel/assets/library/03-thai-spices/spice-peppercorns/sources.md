# Sources — spice-peppercorns (draft ref B4)

Black and white peppercorns, whole and ground. The card deliberately shows both colours equally because the 1954 recipe does not specify which.

## Visual detail -> source

| Drawn detail | Source / tag |
|---|---|
| Whole black peppercorns: dark, wrinkled, net-patterned skin, round | Chanthaburi GI notice: black whole from fully mature berries dried with skin on [P] |
| Whole white peppercorns: round, smooth, pale greyish-cream, cleaner-looking | Chanthaburi GI notice: white whole from ripe berries with skin removed; round smooth corns, cloudy white to brownish/greyish white, natural colour [P] |
| Both colours shown equally, neither flagged as "original" | M.L. Tiw 1954 recipe says only "พริกไทย 20 เม็ด", no colour word; must not claim either colour as original [P] |
| Ground black / ground white powders | Krit: early cook-shop dish "โรยพริกไทยป่น" (ground pepper); ground forms are generic [P] |

## Deliberately left out (unsourced / not visual)

- Which colour is "traditional" for the stir-fry — no primary culinary authority states this; shown even-handedly.
- Chanthaburi GI (พริกไทยจันท์), province, piperine figures — label/text facts, not drawn; handled in HyperFrames.
- Fresh green peppercorns (พริกไทยอ่อน) — not in this card and not sourced this pass.
- Exact corn diameter numbers — not drawn.

## Acceptance criteria (check by eye)

1. Four separate heaps in a row with empty paper between each, none touching.
2. Whole black corns read as dark and wrinkled; whole white corns read as pale and smooth.
3. The two ground powders differ in shade (darker grey-brown vs pale creamy-grey).
4. Black and white are equal in size and prominence — neither dominates.
5. At least a couple of loose single corns show the round shape of one peppercorn.
6. No text, numbers or labels anywhere in the image.
