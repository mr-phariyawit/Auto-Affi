Two kinds of garlic laid side by side on the page for comparison, equal scale, clearly separated with a wide band of empty cream paper between them, not touching.

On the right, the large-bulb type: a single markedly bigger bulb with pinkish papery skin, and three or four broad individual cloves broken off and set beside it, each loose clove plainly larger than the cloves on the left.

Both kinds keep their dry papery wrappers and short trimmed root plates. The drawing shows the papery skin texture, the way the cloves pack into each bulb, and the clear difference in overall bulb size between the two.
