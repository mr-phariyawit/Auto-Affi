# Sources — veg-three-basils (draft ref A2)

Reused verbatim from the independently-audited draft (art-direction-draft.md §4, A2); only the STYLE paragraph was appended. Audit fixes already applied ("purple bracts" and "dense" removed; sweet basil spike described as long and lax, not dense; no Latin name for lemon basil).

## Visual detail -> source

| Drawn detail | Source / tag |
|---|---|
| Holy basil: hairy green stem, broadly elliptical softly hairy lighter-green leaves, sparse shallow teeth | PROSEA *O. tenuiflorum* [P*]; Pailin: holy basil leaves "lighter green, more rounded, more delicate" [S] |
| Holy basil: short loose spike of very small pink flowers | PROSEA: raceme 8–10 cm, lax; flowers 3 mm pink/white [P*] |
| Sweet basil: smooth nearly hairless stem flushed purple, narrower pointed darker leaves, margin smooth to faintly scalloped | PROSEA *O. basilicum*: stem "light green to dark purple", blade ovate–elliptical, margin "entire to crenate-serrate", glabrous to slightly pilose [P*]; Pailin: Thai basil "dark green, pointy leaves… purple stems" [S] |
| Sweet basil: long lax flower spike, larger flowers | PROSEA: inflorescence "up to 30 cm", corolla 5–8 mm [P*] |
| Lemon basil: yellowish-green stem, white hairs on young tips, narrow lance-shaped hairless leaves, completely smooth margins, white flowers in spaced whorls | PROSEA *O. americanum*: stem "yellowish-green, densely white-pilose in young parts", blade lanceolate–elliptical "margin entire", glabrous; corolla white; 6-flowered whorls [P*] |

## Deliberately left out (unsourced)

- Latin binomial for lemon basil (แมงลัก) — sources disagree (*O. americanum* vs *O. × africanum/× citriodorum*); left unnamed on purpose.
- "Glossy" for sweet basil — contradicts the matte STYLE; shown instead as darker, smoother, flatter green.
- "Purple bracts" and dense/crowded flower spike — removed in audit (no source / conflicts with PROSEA "lax").
- Exact stem colour of sweet basil fixed as one shade — source gives a range "light green to dark purple"; drawn as a purple flush.

## Acceptance criteria (check by eye)

1. All three sprigs clearly separate, equal size, equal spacing — none touching or merged.
2. Holy basil (left) shows hairy stem and sparse-toothed, softly hairy leaves.
3. Sweet basil (centre) shows a hairless-looking purple-flushed stem, pointier leaves, smoother margin.
4. Lemon basil (right) shows the narrowest, lance-shaped leaves with completely smooth (untoothed) margins.
5. The three read as three different plants when shrunk to phone size.
6. No text, arrows or labels anywhere in the image.
