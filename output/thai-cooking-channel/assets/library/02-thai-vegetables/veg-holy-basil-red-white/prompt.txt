Two cut sprigs of Thai holy basil (Ocimum tenuiflorum) laid side by side on the page, same size and same pose so they can be compared, each about twenty centimetres long with the cut stem at the bottom, clearly separated with a wide empty gap of paper between them, not touching and not overlapping.

Shared structure for both sprigs: an erect, branching herb stem covered in fine short spreading hairs. Leaves in opposite pairs, each pair set at right angles to the pair below. Each leaf has a distinct slender stalk, and a broadly elliptical blade with a softly hairy surface and a margin of sparse, shallow, widely spaced teeth. At the tip of each sprig, a short, lax flower spike (roughly eight to ten centimetres), made of small separated whorls of tiny flowers.

Left sprig, the white type: stem and leaves pale green throughout, tiny white flowers.
Right sprig, the red type: stem and leaf veins flushed purple-red, leaves green tinged with purple-red, tiny pink flowers.
