Two mounds of raw minced pork shown side by side on the page for comparison, equal scale, clearly separated with empty cream paper between them, not touching. Both are coarsely chopped, so the mince looks hand-cut and uneven, made of ragged short strands rather than a smooth, fine, even grind.

On the left, pork with fat: pink-red meat shot through with many small irregular flecks and short ribbons of soft white fat, the lean and the fat clearly mixed together, giving the pile a paler, marbled, speckled look.

On the right, lean pork: a deeper, more even rosy-red meat with only a trace of white fat here and there, reading as a leaner and more solid colour overall.

Each mound is a loose, open pile of coarse mince, with the individual strands and the fat flecks drawn one by one, so the difference in fat between the fatty pile and the lean pile is easy to see at a glance.
