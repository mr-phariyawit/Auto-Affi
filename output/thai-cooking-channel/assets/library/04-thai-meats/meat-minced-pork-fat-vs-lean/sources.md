# Sources — meat-minced-pork-fat-vs-lean (draft ref B3)

The teaching point is fat level and coarseness of the chop, NOT breed. Thai market pork is ordinary commercial crossbred pig, the same animal sold abroad.

## Visual detail -> source

| Drawn detail | Source / tag |

## Deliberately left out (unsourced / corrected)

- Breed — NOT depicted. Thai commodity pork is Duroc/Landrace/Large White crossbred like pork abroad (Chaosap et al. 2025 [P]); the asset deliberately shows no breed cue.
- Native "black pig" (fatter, darker, redder) — a niche product, not "what Thais normally use"; not drawn.
- Any fat percentage number — no Thai authority gives one; nothing numeric shown.
- Cut name (สันคอ collar / สะโพก ham / สามชั้น belly) — no Thai culinary authority specifies a cut for kra pao; left unshown.

## Acceptance criteria (check by eye)

1. Two separate mince piles with empty paper between them, equal scale, not touching.
2. Left pile visibly contains white fat flecks/ribbons mixed through the lean.
3. Right pile is a more uniform red with clearly less fat.
4. Both piles look coarsely chopped and ragged, not a smooth fine paste.
5. No whole cut of meat, no bone, no packaging, no label.
6. No text, numbers or labels anywhere in the image.
